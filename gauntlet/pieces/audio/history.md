# Piece: audio (music, radio voices, effects, mix)

The user's brief: **80s epic synth** (Vangelis's Blade Runner, Tangerine Dream, John Carpenter, Mass Effect 1), deadly serious stakes against godlike foes, a gritty gallows-humoured crew
(Ghostbusters against Gozer, the Nostromo), never zany. Music composed as MIDI by the agent; the user will swap in their own MP3s later. Radio voices through Trap Door's TTS pipeline
(voice design only, never cloning). Weighty, physical effects with near and far versions. No critic can judge sound: **the user's ears are the judge**, and nobody has heard any of this yet.
Code: `Game/Assets/Monolith/Scripts/Audio` (assembly `Monolith.Audio`, its `README.md`), `Game/Audio/` (the wiring and the self-test), the offline tools in `tools/audio` (its `README.md`),
the score and the voices in `audio_src/`, the built sound in `Game/Assets/StreamingAssets/Audio` (about 18 MB), sources and licences in `audio_src/AUDIO_SOURCES.md`.

## Round 1 (builder, 2026-10-24)

### The method, and why
- **One software mixer, Unity as the sound card.** The game's own mixer (`Dsp/Mixer.cs`: voices, five buses, ducking, a music deck, tone generators, a lookahead limiter) is filled from Unity's `OnAudioFilterRead`.
  Nothing else in the game uses an AudioSource. Because it is plain C# it renders the same in the game, headless in the checks, and **offline in step with a capture run** (800 samples a tick, recorded), which is how the clips with
  sound were made and why every check can run without a sound card. It replaces Unity's mixer and 3D sound, which could not be tested headless, ducked from a sidechain or recorded deterministically. Cost: about 1.4% of a core for the
  music, +2.7% for 40 voices on .NET 9; the player's audio thread (Mono) runs at 3% in the quiet escort opening and 15% to 17% (max 20%) in the dogfight's stress moment with all 64 voices busy.
- **Music is rendered offline from MIDI**, not played by a real-time synth and not made in Python: a C# synth in `tools/audio/Synth` (the DSP is shared with the game, and the Windows Python here has no numpy). Offline buys oversampling-free
  quality at any cost (long reverbs, a master chain), a loop rendered two passes round with the second kept (reverb tails wrap, the seam is exact), loudness set by measurement, and the result is a plain file, so the user's MP3 is the same kind of thing.
  Composition is Python writing standard MIDI (`audio_src/compose`, no packages), one track per instrument named for the synth patch, with expression, filter-sweep and vowel controllers: `audio_src/midi/*.mid` opens in any DAW.
- **The palette** (nothing sampled, nothing General MIDI): Juno-style pad (saw plus PWM pulse plus sub through a 4-pole ladder, chorus II), Oberheim-style warm pad, Solina string ensemble (three-phase chorus), a **CS-80 brass** (two saws and a pulse, the ladder
  filter swells open on a slow envelope and with the expression pedal, a scoop up into the pitch, delayed vibrato, glide: Vangelis), a darker low brass, a Jupiter-style brass stab, a Prophet-style mono lead, a Moog bass, a Carpenter pulse bass, a sine sub with
  saturation, a **Tangerine Dream sequencer** (plucked resonant pulse swept by CC74 over each section) with a dark tape ping-pong echo, an FM bell and electric piano, a **formant choir** (a saw through three vowel filters, five voices, vowel morphing ah to oh),
  a dark drone (low saws, a breathing filter), analog sweeps, and a drum machine in the LinnDrum and Simmons manner (kick, gated snare, clap, electronic toms, metallic hats, crash, taiko, cinematic boom) feeding a **gated reverb** (a bright dense hall, gated for 190 ms),
  with a plate and a hall as the main sends. Each patch is calibrated to the same loudness so 0 dB means the same on every track, and tracks are then fitted to role targets from measured stem loudness (`mixfit.py`).

### The score (all in D minor, 112 BPM, 4/4)
The four loops share **one 32-bar form and one harmony**, so the game changes state by cross-fading aligned loops (a louder state on the next beat with a 0.85 s fade, a quieter one on the next bar over 3.4 s); the mixer keeps a music clock and starts the new
loop at the position the old one has reached. Form: A bars 1-8 (i, VI, iv, V: the lament), B 9-16 (the relative major lifts), C 17-24 (D minor against E flat, the Phrygian second: the dark turn), D 25-32 (the climb back, ending on V so it falls into D minor again). Two themes:
the **call** (A, D, F, then E held: the diaspora's) on brass and choir, and the **lament** (a falling line, A G F E D: what the dead hear). Earth has no theme, only a semitone (D against E flat) and a low horn.

### The voices (`audio_src/voice`: `cast.json`, `collect_lines.py`, `gen_voice.py`, `run_voice.sh`)
Trap Door's Breeze-TTS-2 pipeline, copied and slimmed (Trap Door itself untouched; the WSL install under `~/breeze` is only read). **Voices are designed from text** (age, accent, delivery; dry and deadpan), seed 42, cfg 4; three seeds tried per line and the best kept
(rejecting silent, clipped or runaway takes). 124 clips, 510 s, for 140 combinations of line and speaker, from both mission files and `Resources/AI/radio.json` (every wingman acknowledgement; a line a wingman may say is recorded in each wingman voice).
Cast: Small Mercy's comms officer (a Scottish woman, median pitch 176 Hz), Tamsin's captain (an Irish woman, 223 Hz), Sable 2 (an Australian man, 120 Hz), Sable 3 (a Londoner, 213 Hz), Sable 1 (the player's own, for lines no wingman is left to say; 8 lines), Lantern (a deep Texan, 74 Hz),
**Wick, an alien ally**, and **Earth** in two timbres (Warden's, and the Lector's, an ancient hollow priest-machine; both about 90 Hz before the filter). The radio (`Dsp/Radio.cs`, shared with the game so the player's recordings go through it too):
crews are band-limited (320 Hz to 3.3 kHz), levelled, saturated, with a squelch click and burst in and out and a noise bed; **Earth** is the voice doubled an octave and a fourth down and a fifth up, a metallic comb, a slow amplitude flutter, a cold EQ, preceded by a second of power-up
(a sub swell, shimmer) and followed by a 4.8 s hall; **the alien** is a pitch-shifted double with a ring-modulated buzz, a wandering resonance, a purr and chitters under it, and bubbling chirps for its squelch. Levelled to about -23 LUFS (Earth -21).
The index (`vo/index.json`) maps the line's text (upper case, spaces collapsed) to the clip per voice; at run time the speaker's voice first, then any voice's clip of the same words, then a squelch and a logged warning (`[MN][WARN] VOICE no clip for ...`). The player's own recording of the same name replaces a clip.
Checked by numbers only (pitch and pace per voice, durations, no clipped or runaway takes): whether the accents came out as described is for the user's ears.

### The effects (`tools/audio/Sfx`, all synthesised: oscillators, noise, modal and FM synthesis, comb filters, the shared reverbs; **no recording, no library, no CC0 placeholder**)
58 cues, 110 files (variants and distant versions), peak-normalised then given a level in `sfx.json`, with a minimum distance, a near-to-far crossfade range, an arrival delay for far sounds, a priority and a duck. Guns: the player's fast gun (snap, falling saw zap, pulse body, thump; 3 variants), Earth's gun (ring-modulated, colder),
the heavy gun (a slow wallop with a sub boom and a ringing plate), a capital turret, flak and its burst, missiles and torpedoes (a release clunk, ignition, a hiss that climbs) and their passing hiss as a loop with Doppler, decoys and their spoof, the near miss (a ripping Doppler hiss), beams (charge 2.3 s, a crack, a roaring loop of beating saws, the bite, the fall).
Impacts: shield (a bubble, a sizzle, a ring) and hull (a bar's inharmonic ring, a crack, a thud, shards), each in an on-you version with a cabin boom and rattle, a heavy one, shield-down, a subsystem blast, a collision. Explosions in three sizes, a capital ship's break-up (tearing and sixteen clangs), its shockwave (a sub swell you feel). Cockpit: the engine hum (two detuned saws beating, a sub, air; **pitch follows speed**),
the afterburner roar and its ignition, cabin air, a hull alarm (two tones), four hull creaks (more often and deeper under heavier hits), a system failure, a scan sweeping the hull. The jump flash in and out (small and big), a **deep horn when an Earth ship arrives** (low brass chord, a hard bite then dread, a 5.5 s hall). Interface: click, select, target blip, lock chimes, power, radio squelches.
**The lock tone and the missile warning are generated live** (gated sequencer pings whose rate and pitch follow the lock and the missile's ETA), in the mixer, so they duck and limit with everything else; the HUD's stand-in tones (`CombatAudio`) are silenced through `AudioLink.Owned`.
Juice hookup: `AudioRig` gives the juice an `IJuiceAudio` (`JuiceAudioAdapter`) through `JuiceAudioHooks.Factory` (a 5-line hook in `JuiceDirector.CreateAudio`); four cues were added to the enum (capital turret, torpedo, jump in, jump out) and the weapon table and Jumps use them.

### The mix
Buses: music, effects (normal, big, interface), voice. Player volumes (master 1.0, music 0.75, effects 1.0, voice 1.0 by default; a slider position squared) in `audio.json`, keys F2 to F8, - and =. **The voice ducks music by 9 dB and effects by 4.5 dB** (25 ms in, 600 ms out, measured: -9.0 and -4.5);
**a big moment ducks the rest**: each big cue has an amount and a time (a capital ship's death 55% for 3.5 s, a beam 30%, an arrival 35%; measured in the self-test: the music -15.9 dB under a capital death with the voice also speaking, back within a few seconds); stings dip the music 3.5 dB;
pausing mutes the engine and drops the effects 8 dB. Slow motion lowers the pitch of effects. A lookahead limiter at -1 dBFS (verified exactly against a brute-force reference) ends the master: **zero clipped samples** in every check and run.
**Levels by loudness, not by ear:** loops -26, -24, -22, -20 LUFS integrated, stings -17.5 to -23, endings -21 to -24, radio about -23 (Earth -21), effects by peak and level; a scripted battle (battle 3, 30 sounds a second, explosions, a capital death, a radio line) measures -25.7 LUFS integrated at the default volumes,
loudest 400 ms -20.5 LUFS, peak -8.6 dBFS, the limiter never engaged; the live dogfight reads short-term -18 to -26 LUFS with peaks to -4 dBFS. Stems were fitted by role (lead brass 0, drums -1, bass -1.5 to -5, pads -3 to -6, choir -3, bells -7) in K-weighted stem loudness, with the drone and sub held lower so the bands stay balanced
(octave-band profile of the finished battle loops: 63 Hz the loudest, 125 to 1 kHz 2 to 9 dB under it, 4 kHz about -17, 8 kHz about -21).
A busy battle can ask for fifty sounds in a tenth of a second: each cue may start only so often (a gun stays a rattle), a sound too quiet to hear is never started, the mixer skips voices below -90 dB, and 64 voices are mixed with the least important stolen first.

### How to run
- The playtest: `Playtest\Monolith_2026-10-24_audio\Play.bat <mission>` (`escort` or `dogfight`; `window`; a difficulty), with `SOUND.txt` and `AUDIO_LIST.txt`. The log it leaves (`Monolith_audio_log.txt`) lists every music change, every radio line with its clip, every line without one, and the audio thread's load.
- Remake the sound: `tools/audio/README.md` (music from MIDI about 8 minutes, effects seconds, voices 5 to 25 s a line on the GPU, resumable).
- Checks: `Monolith.exe -batchmode -nographics -mnAudioSelfTest -mnOut dir`; `tools\audio` `mixertest`, `sheet`, `analyse`.

### Least sure of
- **Everything artistic, because nobody has listened.** The synth and mix follow the real machines and the numbers say it is balanced and clean, but whether the CS-80 brass sounds like Vangelis, whether the choir is a choir, whether the gated snare is gated enough, whether the Earth voice is vast and still intelligible, whether the accents came out as described (Breeze follows text descriptions loosely), whether the horn is a Reaper's, whether the guns are meaty: the user's ears decide. Every layer is a patch parameter or a line in `music.json` and `SfxLib.cs`, so changes are quick.
- Loop fatigue: 68 s loops over a 10 to 15 minute mission, with only four states. The first fix would be a second variant of each loop in another key area or with different rhythmic material; the mixer already takes any number of aligned loops.
- The battle loops are dense and heavily compressed (LRA 1.5 to 2.4 LU): drive without a lot of dynamics. The ambient bed is quiet by design (-26) and may need a lift on a small speaker.
- Voices: 124 clips by a text-to-speech model that drawls and sometimes rushes (a few lines run slow); a handful of takes were the best of three seeds, not perfect ones. Wick's alien texture is a processing recipe; it may sound silly.
- The mission's own music rules (hostile counts, `DropAfter`) decide when the intensity changes; the escort mission's first sting and `battle1` fire at 58 s, and Lector's horn arrives together with the battle 3 change at 113 s: the audio follows, it does not choose.
- Trigger rumble and the cabin (breathing, the go-dark moment) are not sounded beyond the scan sweep and dark-cabin hum: the cabin does not exist yet.
- Unity decodes OGG and MP3 for us at start (3.7 s for 241 files; the music is held in memory as 16-bit PCM, about 130 MB); in the player the first radio line can arrive before the sound has loaded (it waits, it is not lost).

### Gotchas found
- `UnityWebRequestMultimedia` needs the `com.unity.modules.unitywebrequest` and `unitywebrequestaudio` packages (added to `Packages/manifest.json`); the first compile after that change reports the type missing, the second is clean.
- Unity refuses an `OnAudioFilterRead` component on an object that also has an AudioListener: the listener has its own object.
- A cue the juice plays with `Play` that is a loop in the catalog (`beam_impact_loop`) must be a one-shot there, or it never ends.
- `ffmpeg`'s Ogg muxer picks a random stream serial, so every encode differs; `-fflags +bitexact` makes regenerating unchanged sound change no bytes in git.
- Offline mode with a recorder grows by 6.4 KB a tick: only a capture asked to record keeps its audio, and headless runs (soaks) have no audio system at all.
