# Piece: human-capitals (Earth's capital ships; the corvette first, the destroyer later)

Owner of Earth's capital-ship models, the art pipeline they come through (`art_src/PIPELINE.md`) and the damage presentation every ship will
use. Bar: The Expanse's space frames, blind, with modern FS2's design language (`reference/critic_pool/capital_ship_close` and `capital_ship_wide`,
both sources). Code: `tools/blender_lib` (kit), `art_src/earth_hull` (textures), `art_src/earth_corvette` (model), `Game/Assets/Monolith/Scripts/ShipArt`
(runtime), `Editor/ShipArtSetup.cs`, scene `"scene": "corvette"` (`Scripts/Game/ShipShowcase.cs`), plans `gauntlet/shots/corvette.json` and
`corvette_damage.json` (made by `tools/ship/make_ship_plan.py`).

## Round 1 (builder, 2026-10-24)

### The pipeline in a few lines
A model script (`art_src/<ship>/build_<ship>.py`) composes a kit in Blender 5.2: hull **lofts** (chamfered-rectangle stations joined by planar bands), **armour
plates** as real chamfered slabs in rows with gaps (some carrying smaller plates), **trim-sheet strips** (windows, light bars, fasteners, conduit) and decals
(hatches, vents, stencils) laid from a generated atlas, rings, fins, lattices, trusses, then **named subsystems** built by part functions. Textures are
generated by numpy (`earth_hull`: 1024 plate tile at 12 m, 2048 trim atlas with 21 pieces, 512 chrome tile, 512 detail map, engine/plume/light emissives, a
2048 damage-decal atlas): no stock images. One FBX plus a JSON manifest per ship; `Setup.Project`/`Setup.All` runs `ShipArtSetup`, which creates the HDRP
material **assets** (the decal and unlit-glow ones too), the prefab with a LOD group, the collision proxy, the subsystem components, and fails if a subsystem
is not where the manifest says (a wrong axis conversion cannot pass). Runtime never uses `Shader.Find`. The scene `"corvette"` uses `SkyRig.Enable` and its
defaults untouched: Sun, fill, fixed EV 14, ACES, bloom, `CameraLook`.

### What the corvette is
The Warden class, 374 m with its engines, 76 m wide over the sensor boom, 91 m tall over the fin: a long layered stack, aft to bow: an engine block with
five recessed glowing bells (centre bell larger) and two ranks of cantilevered radiator louvres, a reactor segment with three chrome heat-exchange stacks
and a flak gallery down each flank, a chrome collar, the main hull (the widest, 56 m) with a shoulder belt to starboard and a shorter, offset one to port,
three stepped dorsal decks carrying the bridge block (lit canopy band), a tall lattice sail fin to port and a low one to starboard, a ventral keel, a
long truss reaching to a sensor dish on the starboard side (the asymmetry), a recessed neck, the forward hull with a comb of spines and two launcher banks,
another neck, and a chrome prow of three lances. Colour is matte black plating in three paint batches, brushed chrome on collars, rails, ribs of the
frame, truss, bells and barrels, cold-white window rows, small red and green navigation lights (red to port, green to starboard) and white strobes.
Hull code `EC-114` is stencilled on the starboard belt. No wedges, no tower, no flat engine discs (the bells are deep funnels with a hot throat and an
additive plume).

Subsystems (32, every one separate, named `SUB_<kind>_<name>`): 8 flak batteries (reactor flanks), 12 anti-fighter guns (decks, keel, forward hull), 5 engines,
2 launcher banks, 2 sensor arrays (fin panel, boom dish), the bridge, a comms mast and the reactor stacks. Each has an intact look, a wreck (scorched stump,
torn plates, embers; the engine bell is split open) and 2 to 6 detachable debris pieces with ejection velocity and spin (117 in all). Plus 24 FX hooks
(sparks, vents, fire), 8 hull damage sites each with a hidden breach mesh, and 65 sampled hull points for the progressive scorch plan.

### Destruction and damage (added mid-task at the user's request)
`ShipDamage` implements `IShipDamage` (full usage in `art_src/PIPELINE.md`): `SetSubsystemHealth` / `DestroySubsystem` swap a part to its wreck and place
its debris at any time after release; `DetachDebris` hands the chunks to the effects system; `AddHullHit(point, normal, radius, severity)` projects a scorch
(HDRP decal, one atlas of small scorches, blasts, craters with glowing cracks, streaks; a pool of 96 projectors) and hurts the nearest damage site;
`SetHullDamage(0..1)` plays a fixed plan of hits, so damage builds as the hull is hit, then opens the sites; `Hooks(kind)` lists spark/vent/fire places. Nothing
here knows hit points: combat drives it. Verified in captures with 8 parts destroyed, 49 decals and 4 explicit hits (`captures/corvette/r1/damage`):
`damage_flank` and `damage_three_quarter` show it best (burnt stumps and debris along the reactor flank, scorched plating, glowing ember craters).

### My read against the reference frames (at the critic's 1280 JPEG; no critic has seen these, the lead picks pairs)
What I looked at: `capital_ship_close/expanse` 0048, 0160; `fs2` big_battle_comparison_0257 and clash_of_titans_ii_0069; `capital_ship_wide/fs2` 0176;
sampled luma on those (pixels above 25/255: mean, median) against ours.
- **What reads right.** Silhouette and scale: it is clearly long and layered, with a recognisable FS2 stack of segments, necks, collars, fins and a
  prow, and next to the placeholder fighter (14 m long) the hull is plainly enormous (`scale_fighter`). The window rows and the small navigation
  lights give the scale the way the FS2 frames' lit rows do, and at 1280 wide they still resolve as rows. The hard Sun plus cold fill gives readable
  planes with real shadows (the plates are geometry, so every step and chamfer catches light: `close_hull`). Exposure sits where the modern FS2 frames do
  (hull mean 56 to 59 against 61 to 70 for clash_of_titans_ii_0069, big_battle_0257 and 0176).
- **The Expanse gap is larger.** Its hulls are pale, painted, uneven plates (mean 89 to 113, median 94 to 128 in 0048 and 0160) with varied tone, worn
  edges and a hot specular line, and almost no regular pattern: the plating is bigger, flatter and irregular, and the camera is softer. Ours is a dark
  charcoal throughout (median 46 to 53), regular in its plate sizes and tone, and uniformly lit. That is the brief (matte black, cold, precise), but a
  critic comparing to the Expanse's pale navy hulls will see "dark, tidy, procedural" first. If the Expanse is what is being judged, Earth's paint wants to
  be a stepped range from near-black to a mid grey with a few light plates and real scuffing at the edges, not three close batches.
- **Biggest material giveaway: the chrome.** It has nothing to reflect (the sky gives only a dim cold fill, no environment), so it renders as pale blue-grey
  plastic and not as mirror metal; the collars, ribs and lances are the brightest large areas in every frame and they look flat. A reflection source
  (a dim baked probe of the Sun and the sky) is the fix, and it belongs to the Sky system, not to this piece.
- **Second: the surface is clean at the middle scale.** There is detail at 12 m (plates), 2 m (windows, hatches) and 0.1 m (grain), but nothing between
  structure and plates: no cable runs, no pipes standing off the hull, no dense machinery. FS2's modern frames fill the same planes with it; ours keep
  large dark flat faces (the aft faces of the reactor stacks, the keel). I held back greebling because the user hates "greebled bricks"; the next round
  should add designed mid-scale structure (trenches, gun housings, cable trays) and not noise.
- **Engine plumes** are smooth additive cones. They read as engines and are bright and cold-white to violet, but they have no turbulence, no
  shock diamonds and a uniform edge: more "icon" than the Expanse's drive flares. Fine for a corvette, not for a hero.
- **Not shown**: the corvette has never been seen with lasers, flak or explosions (the juice system is separate), nor the glare of the Sun across the hull
  (the Sun is 75 degrees off the bow in every shot; none of them has the Sun in frame).
- **Weak points I know of**: the sensor dish at the end of the boom is a plain disc and a grid; the forward-hull spines are thin triangles that look
  stuck on; the bridge canopy is one flat band; the damage decals are readable but their ember cracks look pixelated at 2 m (the atlas cell is 512 px for
  a 10 to 20 m decal); wreck debris is box-like at distance.

## Corvette round 1 verdict (lead, 2026-10-24)
Blind critic vs The Expanse: **lost 3/3** (certain, likely, certain). Pairs: close_hull vs capital_ship_close/expanse/7_ATYOm3nNc_0050; shadow_side vs capital_ship_wide/expanse/7wFt319UB2o_0032; medium_three_quarter vs capital_ship_close/expanse/7_ATYOm3nNc_0048.
- Main giveaway: no key-light structure. The hull reads as one mid grey under a flat ambient wash, with the same fine noise everywhere, and its only contrast comes from emissives (edge strip, truss, identical window rows). The show has one hard sun: bright planes next to deep dark ones, sharp terminators.
- Second: the surface works at one scale. A tiled window-dot pattern and uniform grey, where the show has large-, mid- and small-scale paint and wear variation.
- Third: the engine plumes are long, smooth, even cones "like glowing translucent geometry", too big for the ship, which makes it read as a toy. The show's drives are small, hard, saturated blue-white discs.
- **Conflict to resolve, lead's call:** the user's rule (2026-10-23) is that shadow sides must keep their form and never crush to pure black, which is why the sky has a cool fill. The critic wants "deep black unlit sides". Resolution: a much higher key-to-fill ratio and captures composed with the sun raking the hull. Shadow sides go dark, with only faint form from the fill and rim light, never zero; the sun side carries the structure. The user's rule stands; the fill was simply too strong relative to the key.
- Round 2 gaps: key-to-fill ratio and raking-light composition; large, mid and small-scale paint, wear and panel variation (break up the tiling); plumes small, hot-cored and turbulent; chrome needs something to reflect (environment reflections from the sky).

## Round 2 (builder, 2026-10-24): key light, surface at every scale, small hot engines, reflections

Answers the round 1 verdict point by point. Correction first: round 1's plans used a wrong rotation matrix (pitch backwards), so the Sun in those captures was not at the
(0.31, 0.50, 0.81) the notes gave and the shots sat at other angles to the ship than I thought. Fixed in `tools/ship/make_ship_plan.py`, and the showcase now logs the real Sun
direction in the ship's frame (`SHOWCASE sun in the ship's frame`), which matches the plan (0.50, 0.25, 0.83) to 0.005.

### Lighting (Sky defaults changed, every scene uses them; also recorded in `gauntlet/pieces/sky/history.md`)
- `SkyOptions.AmbientNits` 5,500 to 2,200 and `AmbientAwayFromSun` 0.7 to 1.0. The fill is now 0 facing the Sun and up to twice the base facing away (it was 0.3x to 1.7x), so a sunlit plane
  is about 25 times brighter than the same plane in shadow (it was about 6). `AmbientColor` (0.55, 0.62, 0.78) to (0.58, 0.64, 0.78), a little less blue so polished metal does not tint blue.
  The user's rule holds: shadow sides read 10 to 25 of 255 with form from the fill and the rim of lit edges, never zero (`shadow_side`, median of the ship's pixels 39).
- New `SkyOptions.ScreenSpaceReflections` (on): `SkyRig` adds an SSR override to its look volume and `Hdrp.EnsureAsset` sets `supportSSR` (the asset had it off, which is why
  my first test showed nothing). Cost on the corvette: about 0.1 ms GPU. Nothing else in the look, exposure, bloom or Sun changed.
- Composition: the Sun is now low (elevation 14 degrees), ahead and to starboard of the bow, so it rakes the decks and the starboard flank; the port side, the keel and everything facing aft
  fall dark. Stills: `close_hull`, `medium_three_quarter`, `wide_sun_rake` (from the starboard quarter, more oblique than round 1), `shadow_side` (port quarter, the Sun just out of the top
  of the frame), `scale_fighter` (own plan: the placeholder fighter is only in this shot now), and the new `raking_close` (over the decks, Sun across the plating). Damage set unchanged.

### Surface at three scales
- Large: five paint tones (deep black to pale grey); each hull flat has a dominant tone and its plates follow it with a spread plus accents, so whole stretches of plating are darker or lighter.
- Mid: every plate is its own height; hatches, vents, big inset panels, four stencilled codes, a chevron band; alpha wear overlays chosen by tone (pale dust and scuffs on dark plates, grime, scorch
  and stains on light ones, lighter or darker repainted patches); trenches in the top plating with pipes laid in them; window rows in broken runs of 7 to 46 m with conduit bundles between,
  where round 1 had identical full-length rows; ribs standing 1.7 m proud so they cast real shadows; collars now matte armour with polished lips.
- Small: unchanged (grain, chips, scratches, the tiling detail map), but calmer (the plate normal is a third of round 1's).
- Engine block flanks and top carry a bronze and blue heat tint fading forward from the bells.

### Engines
Bells are about half the size (central: throat 1.7 m, mouth 3.4 m; the others 1.2 and 2.5 m). Inside: a dark funnel with a dull ember glow, and a flat white-blue disc with a hard edge and a saturated rim at the
throat (150,000 nits, it blooms). The flare is short: eight thin nested additive shells (about 4 mouth radii long, white core, fainter blue and violet outside), so its edge falls off in steps too small to see.
`EngineGlow` makes it alive: smooth two-speed noise on brightness and flare length, `Throttle` 0 to 1 (off: no flare, a dark bell). It is a runtime component the flight or effects system can drive.

### Chrome
Less of it, and smoother: collar bands are matte armour with polished lips; the remaining chrome (rails, truss, barrels, stacks, lances, bell lips) is smoothness 0.95 and picks up the Sun as hard glints
and, through SSR, the lit plates beside it. The dish's back is dark ribbed armour with a chrome rim.

## Corvette round 3 verdict (lead, 2026-10-25): one critic per pair
**Lost 3/3, all certain.** r3a raking_close vs capital_ship_wide/expanse/XuqEX1PnG9I_0003 (2:1); r3b close_hull vs capital_ship_close/expanse/7_ATYOm3nNc_0165 (2:1); r3c engine_close vs capital_ship_close/expanse/7wFt319UB2o_0041.

- **Lead's diagnosis:** three rounds of art changes haven't moved "flat lighting", so the cause is in rendering, not paint. **No scene enables any ambient occlusion** (no SSAO, no ray-traced AO), so crevices, recesses and the undersides of overhangs never darken. Each scene builds its own shadow volume (3000 to 6000 m, 4 cascades), so close-ups get coarse shadow maps and lose fine shadowing. Plate textures (1024 tiles) smear at close range.
- **Round 4 is a rendering-quality round, for every scene, not just the corvette.** One shared render-quality profile in the Sky or Look system, replacing the per-scene shadow volumes in ShipShowcase, FlightPlaytest, CombatRange, JuiceSandbox and DogfightScene. It covers: ambient occlusion (SSAO, or ray-traced AO if the RTX 4070 Ti affords it at 60 fps); shadow quality for close-ups at kilometre scale (cascade splits, resolution, filtering, contact shadows, or ray-traced sun shadows if affordable); micro-shadowing and specular occlusion; texel density at close range (higher-resolution trims or detail normal maps at two scales); roughness and specular variation so lit faces aren't one flat value. Then re-judge the corvette with no art change, to isolate the effect.

## Round 4 (builder, 2026-10-25): rendering quality, every scene (no art change in this part)

The round 3 verdict (three critics: "flat and evenly lit", no dark crevices, a small model) was diagnosed in the pipeline, not the paint. I checked the diagnosis against HDRP 14.0.11's source and the defaults the scenes were running on; it was right and worse than stated:

- **Sun shadow maps were 512 pixels per cascade.** `HDAdditionalLightData.k_DefaultShadowResolution` is 512 and `SkyRig` never changed it; the pipeline asset also capped directional maps at 2,048 and filtered at "Medium". With the scenes' 3 to 6 km range and HDRP's default splits (5, 15, 30 per cent) the first cascade ended 150 to 300 m out, so a close-up on a hull 60 m away had 30 to 60 cm texels. That is the blocky, bloated shadow in the r3 captures (a 1.7 m rib throws a staircase).
- **Contact shadows never ran.** The five scene volumes switched them on, but HDRP also asks the light (`useContactShadow`), and the Sun's default is off.
- **No ambient occlusion anywhere** (the SSAO override defaults to intensity 0 and nothing added it), and micro shadows were off.
- Plate textures are 1,024 tiles on 12 m: 1.2 cm a texel, which is not the smear the critics described at 40 to 60 m (a screen pixel there is 3 cm); see "Texel density" in the next part.

### What was built: one shared profile (`Monolith.RenderQuality`, `Scripts/Render/Quality/`)
`RenderQualityOptions` (every field documented, JSON-overridable) and `RenderQuality`, a small component that `SkyRig.Enable` creates beside the Sun: one global volume (priority 6) holding the cascade layout, contact shadows, SSAO and micro shadows, plus the settings on the Sun light that a volume cannot reach. `SkyOptions.Quality` carries the options, so `sky: {"Quality": {...}}` in a showcase plan overrides any of them, and **`-mnQuality '{json}'` or `-mnQuality file.json` on any player run merges over the options of whatever scene is running** (that is how every A/B below was timed, without a rebuild). The five per-scene shadow volumes are gone: `ShipShowcase`, `FlightPlaytest`, `CombatRange` and `JuiceSandbox` lost their `MakeShadowVolume` (their old shadow distances survive as `shadowDistance` options that set `SkyOptions.ShadowDistance`), and `DogfightScene`, which the AI builder owns, lost one line (the `MakeShadowVolume(root.transform);` call: the method below it is now unused and can be deleted by them). Asset side (`Hdrp.EnsureAsset`): directional map cap 2,048 to 4,096, filter Medium to High (PCSS on directional lights), `supportRayTracing` and `supportScreenSpaceShadows` on (needed for the ray-traced options), the HDRP ray-tracing resources assigned in the global settings; the player build lists Direct3D 11 first and Direct3D 12 second (`-force-d3d12`).

| Setting | Before (r3) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Sun cascades | 4 x 512, splits 5 / 15 / 30 per cent of 3 to 6 km | 4 x 4,096, splits 1 / 4 / 16 per cent of 6 km (ending 60 m, 240 m, 960 m, 6 km: about 1.5 cm, 6 cm, 24 cm, 3 m a texel), 10 per cent cascade borders |
| Shadow filter | Medium | High (PCSS) |
| Contact shadows | requested by the scene, never ran at the light | on at the Sun: 1.2 m rays, to 500 m, fading over 100 m |
| Ambient occlusion | none | HDRP SSAO, half resolution, 12 steps, 3 m radius, intensity 2.0, 0.9 of it applied to the Sun's light too (the fill is zero on a lit face, so AO there is only felt if it reaches the key), full specular occlusion |
| Micro shadows | off | on, opacity 1 (the plates' own mask-map occlusion shadows the Sun across fine detail) |
| Ray tracing | n/a | option: `RayTracedAo`, `RayTracedSunShadows` (Direct3D 12 on a DXR GPU; falls back to the above anywhere else) |

### What each feature bought (looked at in the critic's 1280 px JPEG and at 1:1 crops, `gauntlet/captures/corvette/_t` scratch pairs)
- **Shadow maps (4 x 4,096, splits, High filter): the largest change.** Cast shadows are now exact: a rib's shadow on the plate beside it is a sharp parallelogram where r3 had a bloated, stair-stepped smear, window recesses and the undersides of ribs go properly black, and the lit faces keep their light (lit-pixel p90 and p98 do not move, but more of the hull falls into real shadow: the share of the ship over luma 25 drops from 34 to 28 per cent in `raking_close`, 37 to 32 in `close_hull`).
- **Ambient occlusion:** seams between plates, the underside of the hangar rack, the funnels between ribs and the engine collars darken. It is a modest, even effect in the sunlit areas (it only reaches the Sun's light through the 0.9 direct strength) and the largest where the fill is the only light.
- **Contact shadows and micro shadows:** small, real, almost free: a thin dark line under every raised hatch, dome and vent where the shadow maps' bias loses it, and a slight darkening of plate texture relief toward the Sun's grazing angle. Individually hard to see; together they take the "pasted-on" look off small hardware.
- **Specular occlusion** (with the AO): shine in crevices is no longer brighter than the crevice around it (bell collars, rack edges).
- **Ray tracing (compared on the RTX 4070 Ti, Direct3D 12):** ray-traced AO is slightly better than SSAO where the occluder is off-screen or large (the engine block's underside, the rack) and has no screen-space halo or radius limit; ray-traced Sun shadows are not visibly different from the 4,096 maps plus contact shadows at any of the six corvette distances, and noisier in a few penumbra pixels. Neither is the default: Direct3D 12 on this machine costs 30 per cent more frame time in the corvette scene and 70 per cent in the CPU-bound dogfight (188 fps against 319), and the visible gain is small. They are options for a "cinematic" capture mode (`-force-d3d12 -mnQuality '{"RayTracedAo":true}'`; `r4/rt/` holds the six shots with both on).
