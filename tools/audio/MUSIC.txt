MONOLITH - YOUR OWN MUSIC

You can put your own MP3, WAV or OGG tracks in. Anything you do not give a track plays the built-in synthesised music, so you can replace one slot or all of them.

HOW
  1. Put your tracks anywhere: next to Monolith.exe is simplest (a Music folder there is fine).
  2. Open music.json next to Monolith.exe (the one in this folder lists every slot with nothing set) and write a track's name against a slot:
        "battle2": { "file": "Music/my_fight.mp3", "volume": -3 }
     "file" is relative to music.json, or a full path. Leave "file" empty, or take the slot out, and the built-in track plays there.
  3. Play. If the game is running, save music.json and it is read again within a couple of seconds; it takes effect at the next music change.
  The log (Monolith_audio_log.txt next to the game) says what was read ("AUDIO music.json ...: 12 slots, 4 of your files"), names every mistake in the file ("WARN music.json: ..."), and
  lists every music change and theme as it happens ("MUSIC theme lector on (escort/lector <- your file...)").

TWO LAYERS
  BASE   follows the fight, as before: ambient when it is quiet, battle1, battle2, battle3 as more of them are on you. You give each its own track.
  THEMES sit on top of the base, tied to a moment of a mission. The Lector arrives and its theme fades in over the music (a few seconds) and takes over; when the Lector is
         destroyed, or has gone, the theme fades out and the base is back. While a theme plays the base keeps going underneath, ducked (quieter), or silent if the theme "replaces" it, so
         it is still in step when the theme ends. When two themes are on at once the higher "priority" plays and the other waits. Stings (a ship arriving, an objective done or lost) play
         over everything. The ending pieces (victory, failure, death) end every theme.

THE SLOTS
  base     ambient  battle1  battle2  battle3
           A mission's own: put its id first: "escort/battle2" is used in The Crossing only; "battle2" in every mission without its own.
  stings   enemy_arrival  enemy_arrival_battle  friendly_arrival  friendly_arrival_battle  goal_complete  goal_failed
  endings  victory  failure  death
  themes   named mission/theme, or just the theme for every mission. The built-in missions use:
             escort/lector     the Lector arrives (The Crossing): off when it is destroyed
             cabin/lector      the Lector arrives (Dead Water): off when the drones come
             dogfight/destroyer  the Warden's beams charge (The Dogfight): off when its beam turrets are down, or it is
           The Oort mission will use oort/destroyer and oort/outpost. A mission can use any theme name at all: if you have an entry for it, it plays your track; if not, and the
           game has a synthesised theme of that name (lector, destroyer, outpost), that plays; otherwise nothing plays and the log says so once.

WHAT YOU CAN SAY ABOUT A SLOT (all optional)
  file         the track; empty or missing: the built-in sound
  volume       dB; 0 leaves it as it is, -6 is quieter, +3 louder
  fadeIn       seconds to come up           fadeOut   seconds to go away (when it is replaced, or its theme ends)
  loop         true or false: a base track or a theme loops (the default), a sting or an ending plays once
  bpm          the track's tempo, if it starts on the beat (see below);  beatsPerBar (4)
  loopStart, loopEnd   seconds inside the file to loop between (otherwise the whole file; an MP3 starts with a little silence, so a WAV or OGG loops more cleanly)
  themes only: priority (higher wins), mode ("duck" or "replace"), duck (how many dB the base drops by under it, -9)

HOW THE FADES WORK
  Base: a harder state comes in quickly (about 0.85 s) and a quieter one slowly (2.4 s in, the old one out over 3.4 s), and only after the fight has stayed quiet for a few seconds. The first
        ambient fades up over 2.5 s. Victory and failure arrive over about 1.4 s and 2.4 s; death cuts in at once. Your fadeIn and fadeOut replace these for the slot.
  Themes: they fade in over their fadeIn (a built-in theme: 4 s) and out over their fadeOut (6 s), and the base drops by the theme's duck as it comes up and rises as it goes. A theme starts
        together with the bar if it and the music share a tempo, otherwise at once.
  Bar-aware changes: the built-in music is 112 BPM and its changes land on the beat or the bar. If you give a track a "bpm" (and the track really starts on the beat), the music clock takes that
        tempo, and later changes between tracks of the same bpm land on its beat (louder) or bar (quieter). A track with no bpm is a plain cross-fade of the times above.
        To line your loops up in step, make them the same length and bpm (and the base tracks will join each other at the place the one before had reached).
  Stings dip the music 3.5 dB while they sound.

MAKING A MISSION USE A THEME
  The mission files in the Missions folder next to the game can switch a theme on from any event: { "do": "theme", "name": "lector" } turns it on, adding "on": false turns it off
  (and "fadeIn", "fadeOut", "priority", "mode", "duck" change it for that moment). Scripts/Mission/README.md in the source describes the action.

MIXING NOTES
  Aim a track's loudness near the built-in ones (about -24 LUFS for a base track, a little louder for battle3); use "volume" for the rest. The built-in themes sit about 3 dB under the base.
  Everything goes through a limiter, so nothing clips.
