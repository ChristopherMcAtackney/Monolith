MONOLITH - SOUND (audio round 2, 2026-10-24)

Play.bat                   Play.bat escort        Play.bat dogfight window hard
Play.bat with no mission starts Dead Water (the cabin mission). Missions: cabin (Dead Water), escort (The Crossing) and dogfight (The Dogfight). Add "window" for a window and veryeasy/easy/medium/hard/insane for the difficulty.
Nobody has heard any of this yet. It was built, measured and checked by numbers (loudness, spectrograms, a self-test of 104 checks), never by ear: your ears are the judge.

WHAT IS NEW
Everything makes sound now, and all of it was made for this game (nothing from a sound library).
  Music     80s epic synth, composed as MIDI and rendered through an analog-style synth: detuned Juno-style pads with chorus, a Yamaha CS-80 style brass (Vangelis), formant choirs,
            a Moog bass, Tangerine Dream sequencer arpeggios with resonant filter sweeps, a drum machine with gated reverb, big plate and hall reverbs, a deep sub.
            Ambient bed, three battle intensities (they follow how many hostiles are close and on you, as the mission's music rules say), short stings when friends or Earth arrive,
            a sting for a goal done and a goal failed, victory, failure, and your death. The four loops are one 32-bar form at 112 BPM over the same harmony: the music changes on the next
            beat (up) or bar (down) in step with where it was, so it never lurches.
  Voices    every radio line in both missions and every wingman acknowledgement (every line of all three missions, Dead Water's thirteen included, and every wingman acknowledgement: 169 recordings), made with Trap Door's voice pipeline from voice
            *descriptions* (no real person, no cloning) and put through a radio. Accents: Small Mercy's comms officer (South African), Tamsin's captain (Irish), Sable 2 (an Australian man),
            Sable 3 (a Londoner), Sable 1, which is you when no wingman is left to say it (Northern English, Manchester), Lantern (a deep Texan), Wick (an alien: a doubled, ring-modulated,
            whistling voice), and Earth: vast, cold, an octave down with a metallic edge and a long hall, preceded by a power-up swell. No Scottish accent anywhere.
            The voice ducks the music by 9 dB and the effects by 4.5 dB.
  UNDER FIRE THE CREW ARE NOT CALM. Every line is tagged calm, tense or frantic by what fires it and what the music is doing (the table, with the reason for each line, is in
            audio_src/voice/collect_lines.py). Calm: the quiet opening, the briefing, the end. Tense: orders and relief under fire. Frantic: bad news and danger (a wingman lost, the Mercy hit,
            bombers inbound, the Mercy gone). A tense or frantic line is asked of the voice model as a strained, fast, breathless read (and written with a shouter's punctuation), then squeezed of
            its pauses, sped up, given breaths (a sharp breath in before it, a gasp in the longest gap, a ragged breath out after a shout), and sent through a harder radio: more distortion and a
            little clipping on the shouting, a louder noise bed, a squelch that snaps open and shut, a few brief drop-outs as the signal breaks up. It is also louder (calm -23, tense -21.5,
            frantic -19.5 LUFS). The wingmen's acknowledgements exist in a tense and a frantic take and the game plays the one the music asks for: tense in the quiet and at first contact,
            frantic in the fight (battle 2 and 3). Earth never panics. 43 calm, 52 tense and 74 frantic recordings.
            A mission whose lines nobody has voiced now fails the checks (a line with no recording plays as a squelch: clicks and no voice, which is what Dead Water did in the cabin playtest).
            To hear the difference without playing: frantic_reel.mp3 next to Play.bat (52 s: six lines, each as the old calm read, then the tense one where there is one, then the frantic one).
  Effects   guns (a snap, a falling zap, a thump), the heavy gun, capital turrets, flak, missiles and torpedoes with their hiss as they pass, decoys, the lock tone and the missile warning (sequencer pings),
            shield and hull hits (heavier and with a cabin boom when they are on you), explosions in three sizes, a capital ship's break-up, death and shockwave, beams (charge, crack, roar, the bite, the fall),
            the engine hum (its pitch follows your speed) and the afterburner roar, cabin air, hull alarms when you are hurt, creaks under hits, the jump flash, and a deep horn when an Earth corvette or
            capital ship arrives. Distant versions of the big sounds are duller, smeared and late. The biggest moments (a capital ship dying, a beam firing) pull everything else down for a few seconds.

  The cabin  (stand with View, walk, pull the breaker, repair, load the rack: every sound is the room, the ship or your body; there are no hands in the view, so no hand sounds)
            Footsteps: a boot on steel deck plate, grating or a rung, a heel and a toe, the plate ringing over the void under it, and a low thrum from the gravity plating that gives under your weight and settles.
            The harness opens and closes. The breaker: the lever grinding down its slot (from the press), the heavy clunk, then the ship powering down (a whine falling away, relays dropping out) or booting
            (relays chattering in turn, a climbing whine, the four displays coming up one after another). In the dark: the emergency lighting's transformer hum swells, your own breathing comes up (louder the darker it is, and
            louder again while a scan crosses), and a heartbeat that quickens when a scan is on you. A scan crossing the hull is a bowed-steel moan that follows the beam from side to side, then a bong through the hull when it finds you
            or a moan that falls away when it misses. Hull creaks. The repair panel's hatch letting go of its seal, then living tech knitting into the hull under the repair (it climbs in pitch as the repair progresses), a chime when
            it takes, a hard slip when a hit shakes the work. The weapon rack: the latch, the servo hauling the module out, the module coming free, then the heavy clack, the servo driving the new one home, the latch and a hiss of seals.

WHAT TO LISTEN FOR AND TELL US
  - Does the music feel like Blade Runner / Carpenter / Mass Effect, and does it stay out of the way of the radio? Do the changes between intensities feel right, or too eager or too late?
  - Is any loop tiring? (Each is 68 s. Tell us which, and we will lengthen it or add variants.)
  - Are the guns meaty? The heavy gun? Is anything too loud or too quiet against the rest: guns, hits, explosions, the horn, the alarm?
  - Is every radio voice clear enough, and does it sound like who it is meant to be? Earth's voice: vast and cold, or muddy? Wick: alien, or silly?
  - Do the crew sound properly panicked in a fight and calm only in the quiet? Too much distortion on the shouting, or too little? Do the wingmen's "Copy, going in" sound like a pilot under
    fire? (Listen in the dogfight: the Mercy's calls and Sable 2 and 3 losing each other are the frantic ones.)
  - Is the engine hum something you stop hearing (good) or hate (bad)? The alarm: urgent or annoying?
  - The cabin: do the footsteps feel like a person on a steel deck? Is the breaker a satisfying heavy clunk, and does the power going down and coming back sound like a ship? In the dark, is the breathing close and a little frightening, or too loud?
    Does the scan through the hull tell you where the beam is?
  - Do the explosions sound like they have the right weight in a vacuum?

YOUR OWN SOUND
  The Audio folder next to Monolith.exe takes MP3, WAV or OGG files and plays them instead of ours, live (no restart: save the file, it swaps in within about two seconds; delete it and ours comes back).
  AUDIO_LIST.txt lists every sound that can be replaced and what to name it. Music: music\battle2.mp3 and so on, or the names a mission gives its tracks (The Crossing names
  crossing_ambient, crossing_battle_1 .. 3: put music\crossing_battle_2.mp3 there and it plays in that mission). A loop that is exactly 68.571 s joins the others in step; any other length
  starts from its beginning on the next bar. Add a file of the same name with .json for loop points: { "loopStart": 3.2, "loopEnd": 48.0, "volume": -2.5 }.
  Radio lines: vo\<voice>\<line>.wav; your recording goes through the game's radio. Add { "radio": false } in a .json beside it if it is already radio-ready.
  A line made tense and frantic has a file for each (..._tense, ..._frantic); a recording named for one is given that radio treatment (the frantic one gets the harder radio).
THE LOG
  Monolith_audio_log.txt next to the game lists every music change, every radio line and the clip it played ([MN] VOICE ...), every line that had no clip ([MN][WARN] VOICE no clip ...), and every
  five seconds the number of voices, the audio thread's load and the loudness. Send it with your notes.
