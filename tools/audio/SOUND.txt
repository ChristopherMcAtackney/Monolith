MONOLITH - SOUND (audio round 1, 2026-10-24)

Play.bat <mission>         Play.bat escort        Play.bat dogfight window hard
Missions: escort (The Crossing) and dogfight (The Dogfight). Add "window" for a window and veryeasy/easy/medium/hard/insane for the difficulty.
Nobody has heard any of this yet. It was built, measured and checked by numbers (loudness, spectrograms, a self-test of 86 checks), never by ear: your ears are the judge.

WHAT IS NEW
Everything makes sound now, and all of it was made for this game (nothing from a sound library).
  Music     80s epic synth, composed as MIDI and rendered through an analog-style synth: detuned Juno-style pads with chorus, a Yamaha CS-80 style brass (Vangelis), formant choirs,
            a Moog bass, Tangerine Dream sequencer arpeggios with resonant filter sweeps, a drum machine with gated reverb, big plate and hall reverbs, a deep sub.
            Ambient bed, three battle intensities (they follow how many hostiles are close and on you, as the mission's music rules say), short stings when friends or Earth arrive,
            a sting for a goal done and a goal failed, victory, failure, and your death. The four loops are one 32-bar form at 112 BPM over the same harmony: the music changes on the next
            beat (up) or bar (down) in step with where it was, so it never lurches.
  Voices    every radio line in both missions and every wingman acknowledgement (140 line and speaker combinations, 124 recordings), made with Trap Door's voice pipeline from voice
            *descriptions* (no real person, no cloning) and put through a radio: Small Mercy's comms officer (a Scottish woman), Tamsin's captain (an Irish woman), Sable 2 (an Australian man),
            Sable 3 (a Londoner), Sable 1, which is you when no wingman is left to say it, Lantern (a deep Texan), Wick (an alien: a doubled, ring-modulated, whistling voice), and Earth: vast, cold,
            an octave down with a metallic edge and a long hall, preceded by a power-up swell. The voice ducks the music by 9 dB and the effects by 4.5 dB.
  Effects   guns (a snap, a falling zap, a thump), the heavy gun, capital turrets, flak, missiles and torpedoes with their hiss as they pass, decoys, the lock tone and the missile warning (sequencer pings),
            shield and hull hits (heavier and with a cabin boom when they are on you), explosions in three sizes, a capital ship's break-up, death and shockwave, beams (charge, crack, roar, the bite, the fall),
            the engine hum (its pitch follows your speed) and the afterburner roar, cabin air, hull alarms when you are hurt, creaks under hits, the jump flash, and a deep horn when an Earth corvette or
            capital ship arrives. Distant versions of the big sounds are duller, smeared and late. The biggest moments (a capital ship dying, a beam firing) pull everything else down for a few seconds.

WHAT TO LISTEN FOR AND TELL US
  - Does the music feel like Blade Runner / Carpenter / Mass Effect, and does it stay out of the way of the radio? Do the changes between intensities feel right, or too eager or too late?
  - Is any loop tiring? (Each is 68 s. Tell us which, and we will lengthen it or add variants.)
  - Are the guns meaty? The heavy gun? Is anything too loud or too quiet against the rest: guns, hits, explosions, the horn, the alarm?
  - Is every radio voice clear enough, and does it sound like who it is meant to be? Earth's voice: vast and cold, or muddy? Wick: alien, or silly?
  - Is the engine hum something you stop hearing (good) or hate (bad)? The alarm: urgent or annoying?
  - Do the explosions sound like they have the right weight in a vacuum?

YOUR OWN SOUND
  The Audio folder next to Monolith.exe takes MP3, WAV or OGG files and plays them instead of ours, live (no restart: save the file, it swaps in within about two seconds; delete it and ours comes back).
  AUDIO_LIST.txt lists every sound that can be replaced and what to name it. Music: music\battle2.mp3 and so on, or the names a mission gives its tracks (The Crossing names
  crossing_ambient, crossing_battle_1 .. 3: put music\crossing_battle_2.mp3 there and it plays in that mission). A loop that is exactly 68.571 s joins the others in step; any other length
  starts from its beginning on the next bar. Add a file of the same name with .json for loop points: { "loopStart": 3.2, "loopEnd": 48.0, "volume": -2.5 }.
  Radio lines: vo\<voice>\<line>.wav; your recording goes through the game's radio. Add { "radio": false } in a .json beside it if it is already radio-ready.
THE LOG
  Monolith_audio_log.txt next to the game lists every music change, every radio line and the clip it played ([MN] VOICE ...), every line that had no clip ([MN][WARN] VOICE no clip ...), and every
  five seconds the number of voices, the audio thread's load and the loudness. Send it with your notes.
