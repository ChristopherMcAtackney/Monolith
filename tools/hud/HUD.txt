MONOLITH: THE NEW HUD
=====================

Play.bat [mission] [halo|meridian|cipher] [window] [veryeasy|easy|medium|hard|insane]

   Play.bat                       The Crossing (escort), the style you last chose (Halo the first time)
   Play.bat dogfight cipher       The Dogfight with the Cipher style
   Play.bat cabin meridian        Dead Water (you start standing at the plan table) with Meridian

You pick a style by playing with it. Change it at any moment: F9 (keyboard), or Start for the pause screen and then D-pad up or down (H on the
keyboard). The game remembers your choice in hud.json beside Play.bat. The same switch is on every pause screen: the dogfight, the flight test, the
range and the missions.


THE THREE STYLES
----------------
All three show the same readings in the same places. They differ in how they look, so you can say which one you read best.

HALO     amber. Oxanium type (squared, a little alien). The two gauges are thick bands cut into cells like a battery, close in round the
         reticle (radius 232 px at 1080p, 21 px thick). The plainest of the three apart from Meridian: almost no ornament.
MERIDIAN pale cyan. B612 type, which was designed for aircraft cockpit screens, so it is the one drawn for being read at a glance. The gauges are long,
         continuous and thinner (292 px out, 13 px thick) with a fine scale outside them like an instrument bezel. No alien ornament at all.
CIPHER   green-teal. Jura type (light, rounded-square, a little like writing). The gauges are heavy plates (256 px out, 28 px thick) with a row of
         the aliens' glyphs along their outer edge and beside each heading. The glyphs are ornament only: every reading is a plain number or word.
         This is the one closest to the Independence Day invaders' glowing consoles.

The warning colour is the only accent: red-orange on Halo and Cipher, orange on Meridian. It means danger: a missile coming at you, a lock on you,
a lost system, low hull, low energy. Nothing else is ever in that colour.

If the type is too small or too big on your screen, add  -mnHudText 1.2  (1.0 is the design size; 0.7 to 1.6) to the last line of Play.bat, or put
"textScale": 1.2 in hud.json.


WHAT IS WHERE (1920 x 1080; it scales to any shape of screen)
-------------------------------------------------------------
THE TWO MAIN BARS, curved, either side of the reticle, filling from the bottom:
   LEFT   your speed (the thick band; its top is the afterburner's top speed, and the mark across it is where cruising ends, with the afterburner's range
          past the mark drawn dimmer) and your throttle setting (the thin band beside it, on the same scale: the thick band chases the thin band's marker;
          ticks at the D-pad's thirds). The big number is the speed in metres a second; the throttle is a percentage under it.
   RIGHT  your weapon energy (thick band, big number) and your afterburner fuel (thin band, a percentage under the number). "BURNING" while the
          afterburner is lit; "FUEL OUT" when the tank is dry; the energy goes to the warning colour below 15 percent.
   Why these pairs: speed and throttle are what you ask for and what you get; energy and fuel are the two things you spend. And each pair sits
   where a glance sideways finds it without taking your eyes off the fight.
RETICLE in the middle, and the lead pip where to aim the fast gun at your target.
TARGET BOX around the target where it is, with its name and range beside it; the part you have picked has its own ring. Off screen, a pointer on a
   ring round the reticle shows which way to turn and how far away it is.
LOCK: while a lock-on missile builds its lock a diamond closes in on the target with the percentage; "LOCKED" on a solid tag when it is made.
TARGET CARD (top right): friend or hostile and its size, name, class, hull and shield in cells, range and speed, the part you picked and its health,
   and the LOCK row: the progress in cells, a small tone mark that beats at the rate the lock tone beeps (quickening as the lock builds) and holds
   steady when the lock is made, and the word (SEEKING, LOCKED; for a heat-seeker, SEEKER when it sees the target).
RADAR (bottom centre), like FreeSpace 2's: you are at the centre facing up the screen; a blip's stem rises when it is above you and drops when it is
   below; a hollow diamond is a friend, a solid one an enemy, a boxed one a capital ship, a triangle a missile (it blinks in the warning colour when it
   is coming for you). The range steps up and down by itself. With your sensors damaged the blips shiver and drop out and static fills the disc;
   with the sensors gone it shows only static and the target card says NO SENSORS.
SHIELD and HULL: the two short thick arcs either side of the radar, with their numbers. POWER under the radar: BAL, SHD, GUN, ENG, the choice underlined.
GUNS AND MISSILES (bottom right): your two guns, your missile types with the one selected marked and its reload shown, and your decoys.
SYSTEMS (bottom left): what is hurt: sensors, engines, a gun mount, the launchers, the reactor (in the warning colour when gone), and MENDING while the
   living tech knits the hull.
DIRECTIVES (left) what to do now; ticked when done, crossed when failed. RADIO (top centre) the speaker and what they say. NOTICES (top left) short
   messages that fade.
INCOMING MISSILE: a plate at the top with the distance and the seconds left, a pointer on a ring round the reticle toward it (brackets round the
   reticle when it is dead ahead), the frame of the screen flashing in the last two and a half seconds, and the warning tone.

THE QUICK MENU (hold Y): power on the D-pad (up guns, left shields, right engines, down balanced) and the wingman orders on A (attack my target),
   B (disable my target), X (cover me) and LB (form up). The current choice is marked. On the keyboard it shows the keys (5 to 8, F1 to F4).
THE BATTLE MAP (View twice quickly, or B on the keyboard; in a mission with the cabin it is the same double tap of View): the whole area as a retro
   map, no 3D ships, a scan line sweeping round, a key to the symbols at the top right. The game does NOT pause, and the radio, the directives, the
   notices and the missile warning stay up. Press the same again to close it.
PAUSE (Start): resume, fly again, quit; the difficulty for the next run; and under SETTINGS the two invert switches (LB flips flight pitch, RB flips
   how you look round the cabin; I and O on the keyboard; both are inverted by default and saved to controls.json beside Play.bat) and the HUD style.


WHAT WAS DONE ABOUT THE NOTES
-----------------------------
"A curved thicker bar for the two main bars, like arcs of a circle": the two gauges are 13 to 28 px thick curved bands, segments of a circle round the reticle.
"The font is all squished up and hard to read": the old type was a hand-drawn four-by-eight stroke font. All text is now a real typeface (a signed-distance-field
   atlas, so it is crisp at any size and screen), at four sizes (small tracked capitals, running text, readings, big numbers) with the smallest
   13 px tall in capitals (19 px to the em) at 1080p, and room between the letters. Mixed case is used for sentences (the radio, the directives).
"More refined overall": one colour, a restrained glow, a dark keyline round everything so it reads over a bright hull, and nothing in the middle of the screen.
The cabin's own screens (the dash, the systems panel, the comms, the plan table's map, the weapon rack, the breaker and the repair panels) use the same
typeface and style, whichever one you picked.


FONTS AND LICENCES
------------------
Oxanium, B612 and Jura are free fonts under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Their licence texts are in the Licences folder beside this file. The game ships
them as baked distance-field atlases, not the font files.


CHECKS (for the curious)
------------------------
Monolith.exe -batchmode -nographics -mnHudSelfTest   89 checks: the type, the data the HUD reads, the layout of every style at eight screen shapes, the pause screens
Monolith.exe -batchmode -nographics -mnScene range -mnPadDrive   30 checks, including the map switch, the invert switches and the style switch with a virtual pad
-mnHudCost logs the HUD's own CPU time per frame.
