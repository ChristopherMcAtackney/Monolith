# Art pipeline: scripted ships, Blender to Unity

Every ship, station and the cabin are modelled **by Python script in Blender 5.2** (`art_src\<ship>\build_<ship>.py`), never by hand
and never from the Unity Asset Store. Textures are generated by script too. Free CC0 textures are allowed only as placeholders, and the
user dislikes obviously stock looks, so none are used. The first ship through it is Earth's corvette (`art_src\earth_corvette`).

```
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\blender.ps1 art_src\earth_hull\build_earth_hull_textures.py     # ~45 s; `-- plate trim` rebuilds some
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\blender.ps1 art_src\earth_corvette\build_earth_corvette.py      # ~5 s; `-- --preview` renders Workbench views
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\unity.ps1 Monolith.EditorTools.Setup.All                       # import, materials, prefab, player build (~20 s)
python tools\ship\make_ship_plan.py corvette --check                                                                      # capture plans in the ship's own frame
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\capture.ps1 -Plan gauntlet\shots\corvette.json -Out gauntlet\captures\corvette\rN
python -I tools\sky\critic_view.py gauntlet\captures\corvette\rN                                                           # the critic's 1280 px JPEGs
```

## What lives where

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `tools\blender_lib\` | the kit, imported by every model script: `mntex` (numpy textures, trim atlas, PNG writer), `mnmesh` (meshes as arrays with explicit normals and two UV sets), `mnkit` (hull lofts, armour plating, trim strips, rings, fins, trusses, lights), `mnparts` and `mnsystems` (turrets, engines, sensors, comms, reactor, launchers, bridge: each with a wreck and debris), `mnship` (LODs, subsystems, hooks, damage sites, collision, FBX and manifest export), `mnpreview` (Workbench renders) |
| `art_src\<set>\build_*_textures.py` | a texture set (Earth's hull: plate, trim sheet, chrome, detail, lights, engine, plume, damage decals) and `trims.json`, the atlas rectangles the model scripts read |
| `art_src\<ship>\build_<ship>.py` | the model: composes kit calls, nothing hand-placed |
| `Game\Assets\Monolith\Art\<Set>\`, `...\<Ship>\` | output PNGs, `*.materials.json` (HDRP recipes), the FBX. Imported by `ShipArtImport` (suffix decides the settings) |
| `Game\Assets\Monolith\Resources\ShipArt\` | `<id>.ship.json` (manifest), `<id>.prefab`, `Materials\*.mat`: written by the model script and `ShipArtSetup`, loaded by `ShipArtCatalog` |
| `Game\Assets\Monolith\Scripts\ShipArt\` | runtime: `ShipSubsystem`, `ShipDamage` (`IShipDamage`), `ShipFxHook`, `HullDamageSite`, `ShipArtCatalog`, `ShipManifest` |

Never put `.blend` files under `Game\Assets`. Keep big sources out of git (over about 50 MB, ask the lead): the corvette set is 23 MB of PNG and a 5.5 MB FBX.

## How a ship is built (the hierarchy of scale)

Capital ships must read as enormous, so detail exists at every scale and is **designed, not noise**:
silhouette (10 to 100 m: lofted hull segments of chamfered rectangles joined by recessed necks and chrome collars, belts, decks, fins, trusses, radiator stacks, lances) ->
structure (2 to 10 m: armour plates in rows with gaps, each at its own height, some carrying smaller plates; ribs; turrets; nozzles) ->
detail (0.2 to 2 m: lit window rows, hatches, vents, conduits, fastener lines, light bars, stencils, running lights, all laid from the trim sheet) ->
surface (under 0.2 m: plate tile paint, scratches, chips, the tiling detail map on UV1 at 2 m).
The plates are real geometry with a chamfer, so every edge catches the Sun and panel lines are depth, not paint.

- **Axes while building:** x starboard, y bow, z up (Blender). `mnship.to_unity` is the one place that flips to Unity's (x right, y up, z forward). FBX settings are fixed by a probe (`mnship.py` top): Blender's axes untouched, no baking, Unity's *Bake Axis Conversion* on. `ShipArtSetup` fails loudly if a subsystem's position or facing in the prefab differs from the manifest.
- **UVs:** UV0 is metric tile coordinates for tiling materials (plate, chrome), an atlas rectangle for trim pieces. UV1 is always metric, for the detail map. Normals are explicit (flat faces own their vertices; lathes carry their own).
- **Materials (slots in the model script's list):** plate in five paint tones (deep black, graphite, mid, light and pale grey: five albedo tiles that share one normal and mask, so chips and scratches stay bare metal on every tone), trim (atlas: windows, hatches, vents, big panels, stencilled codes, chevrons, labels, edge paint, burnt metal), chrome (brushed tile, smoothness 0.95), lights (palette), engine (the dark funnel and the hot disc), plume (HDRP Unlit, additive), wear and paint (HDRP Lit, alpha blended). HDRP Lit mask map: R metallic, G occlusion, B detail mask, A smoothness. Albedo textures hold final values; the tone is in the texture, not in a tint.
- **Paint and wear at every scale (round 2):** `Kit.tones` gives each hull flat a dominant tone and its plates follow it with a spread plus a few accents (large scale: blocks of lighter and darker plating); every plate is its own height and takes its own part of the 12 m tile (mid scale); `Kit.scatter_details` puts a hatch, vent, stencilled code, chevron band or big access panel on most plates; `Kit.wear` lays alpha overlays one centimetre above a plate (grime or pale dust streaks along the ship, scorch blooms, edge scuffs, repainted patches, stains) chosen by the plate's tone (pale marks on dark paint, dark marks on pale paint); `trenches` leave channels in the plating with pipes in them; window rows are laid in broken runs with conduit between. No noise greebles.
- **Paint ageing by material and scale (round 3):** a second alpha-blended atlas, `earth_paint_albedo` (material `MN_EarthPaint`, trims.json key `paint`), holds paint that has aged *with* its coat: the stencilled codes, chevron band, hull number and emblem are solid pale paint that thins and chips (more at the strokes' edges), with primer ringing the holes and grime running below; none is an opaque rectangle with a border any more (`Kit.paint_quad`, laid over the plate). `Kit.edge_wear` lays a chip strip and a grime strip along each plate edge (chips are flakes of top paint gone to the primer with a scrape of bare metal inside, many at the edge and thinning inward, a worn metal line along it; grime gathers at the edge with short drips; dark plates show their chips, light plates collect grime). Plate chamfers and walls (the `edge_wall`, `edge_chamfer` trim strips) wear on their own: grime foot, primer then metal toward the lip. The wear atlas gained `bleach` (sun-bleached warm blotches on mid and dark paint); repaint patches are rare, soft-edged, roller-streaked and thin at one side (they used to be flat rectangles). Hardware pieces (hatches, vents) are aged too. `Kit.tones` keeps the tone spread narrow so the paint does not compete with the Sun's light and dark break.
- **Engine light on the hull (round 3):** the model script lists `ship.extra["engineLights"]` (per engine: distance behind the throat along the exhaust, candela, range); `ShipArtSetup` builds an HDRP point light `SPILL` (cold blue-white, no shadows) under each engine and `EngineGlow` drives it with the same flicker and `Throttle` as the flare (off when the engine is a wreck). The light falls on the engine block's flanks and sterns and the bell collars; it needs no probe or lightmap.
- **LODs:** `<id>_LOD0..2` built by the same function with `ctx.lod`: LOD0 everything; LOD1 carcass, strips and silhouette pieces without plate slabs (the subsystems keep their LOD0 meshes); LOD2 boxes. Screen heights in the manifest.
- **Collision:** `<id>_COL`, a few hundred triangles of coarse hull boxes (the prefab makes it a `MeshCollider`).
- **Check:** `Ship.export` refuses a mesh whose triangle winding disagrees with its normals.

## Subsystems, destruction and damage (the contract with combat)

Every part is its own named object, found by `ShipSubsystem` (kind, radius, hp, tags from the manifest):

```
SUB_<kind>_<name>                 pivot empty; +z is where it points. kinds: engine flak aaf launcher bridge sensors comms reactor
  __base, __head ...              the intact look (several meshes where parts move)
  __damaged                       optional half-wrecked look
  __destroyed                     the wreck: scorched stump, torn plates, embers
  __debris_NN                     detachable chunks, each with ejection velocity and spin in the manifest
FX_<kind>_<NN>                    where sparks, vents, fire and smoke may be spawned (ShipFxHook), +z out of the hull
DMG_site_<NN> (+ __breach)        a place on the hull that can be torn open (HullDamageSite); the breach mesh is hidden until hit
```

Engines are alive: `EngineGlow` (added to every `engine` subsystem by `ShipArtSetup`) flickers the hot core and the flare with smooth noise and scales the flare with `Throttle` (0 off, 1 full); the flight or effects system sets it. The flare is a part of its own (`__plume`) so a wreck loses it.

Combat calls `IShipDamage` (on the prefab root, `ShipDamage`), it needs no knowledge of the model:

```csharp
var dmg = shipObject.GetComponent<ShipDamage>();
dmg.SetSubsystemHealth("SUB_flak_S02", 0.3f);        // below 0.5: damaged look if the model has one; 0: wreck
dmg.DestroySubsystem("engine");                      // by name, or the first intact part of that kind
foreach (var (piece, v, spin) in dmg.Find("SUB_engine_C").DetachDebris()) { /* effects own the pieces now */ }
dmg.AddHullHit(worldPoint, worldNormal, radiusMetres, severity01);   // scorch decal (pooled HDRP decal projectors), opens the nearest site
dmg.SetHullDamage(0.6f);                             // progressive: a fixed plan of sampled hull points, more and bigger scorches, then breaches
foreach (var hook in dmg.Hooks("spark")) { /* spawn particles at hook.transform, along hook.Direction */ }
dmg.SubsystemChanged += (sub, from, to) => { /* explosion, radio line, objective */ };
```

Scorch decals come from one atlas (4 x 4: small scorches, blasts, craters, streaks). Damaged-state frame cost on the corvette: about 3% (see the piece history).

## Adding a ship

1. Copy `art_src\earth_corvette`, rename. Reuse `earth_hull` textures for Earth ships; a new faction gets its own `art_src\<set>` script (copy it, change the look: albedo, trim pieces, markings, emissive colours).
2. Describe the hull as stations (`station(y, hw, hh, chamfer)`), plate each band (`plate_bands`), add rows of windows, collars, fins, then subsystems with `ctx.subsystem(name, kind, frame, radius)` and the part builders. Add a new part builder to `mnparts`/`mnsystems` with an intact look, a wreck and debris: it will be there for the next ship.
3. Run the model script, `Setup.All`, a capture. Look at `--preview` Workbench renders first: they take 10 s and show silhouette and layering.
4. Write the ship's `monolith-ship-class/1` flight data separately (`Resources\Ships\<id>.json`): the model gives only size, subsystems and lights.

## Gotchas

- Blind look-test stills must carry no HUD: the `corvette` scene builds none (no `FlightHud`, no `CockpitView`); keep it that way for any new showcase scene.
- A heredoc with big Python in the bash tool can fail to parse: write patch scripts to a file.
- The capture scene `"scene": "corvette"` (`ShipShowcase`) takes `sceneOptions` for any ship id: orientation (the Sun is fixed in the sky, so yaw/pitch/roll decide the light; `make_ship_plan.py` solves them and the player logs `SHOWCASE sun in the ship's frame` so a wrong matrix shows at once), a placeholder fighter, damage, and `sky` (any `SkyOptions` field by name, for look development without a rebuild). It uses `SkyRig` as the game does and builds no HUD.
- Lens ghosts (`LookOptions.Ghosts`) are the Sun reflected between lens surfaces, so `CameraLook` fades them with the Sun's distance outside the frame (`GhostReach`, 0.45: gone by half a frame beyond the edge). In ship shots with the Sun out of the picture they were sprites that matched nothing lighting the hull; with the Sun in or near the picture they are unchanged.
- Unity applies the textbook rotation matrices to its axes (`Quaternion.Euler` = Ry * Rx * Rz, +pitch points the nose down): `make_ship_plan.rot` follows that. Round 1's plans had Rx backwards, so its Sun was not where its notes said.
- `MaterialPropertyBlock.SetColor` gamma-converts a value, which turns 150,000 nits into white: set emissive nits with `SetVector` (EngineGlow does).
- Screen-space reflections (`SkyOptions.ScreenSpaceReflections`; needs `supportSSR` in the HDRP asset, which `Hdrp.EnsureAsset` sets) are what make polished metal reflect the lit plates beside it. Changing a model changes the juice's break-up sections: run `tools/unity.ps1 Monolith.EditorTools.JuiceSetup.BakeSections` after `Setup.All`.
- Emissive: nits at fixed EV 14 (`earth_hull.materials.json`): windows 42,000, lights 150,000, engine disc 150,000, plume 48,000 (the core column at full strength, the shells outside it a fifth to a twentieth), decal embers 14,000. Bloom starts around 17,700 nits.
- HDRP decal emissive and DBuffer need nothing in the player settings, but the decal material must be an asset (it is: `MN_EarthDamageDecal`).
- `_UVDetailsMappingMask` is set by the material inspector in the editor, not by `ResetMaterialKeywords`: `ShipArtSetup` sets it itself.
