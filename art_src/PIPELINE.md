# Art pipeline: scripted ships, Blender to Unity

Every ship, station and the cabin are modelled **by Python script in Blender 5.2** (`art_src\<ship>\build_<ship>.py`), never by hand
and never from the Unity Asset Store. Textures are generated by script too. Free CC0 textures are allowed only as placeholders, and the
user dislikes obviously stock looks, so none are used. The first ship through it is Earth's corvette (`art_src\earth_corvette`).

```
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\blender.ps1 art_src\earth_hull\build_earth_hull_textures.py     # ~45 s; `-- plate trim` rebuilds some
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\blender.ps1 art_src\earth_corvette\build_earth_corvette.py      # ~5 s; `-- --preview` renders Workbench views
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\unity.ps1 Monolith.EditorTools.Setup.All                       # import, materials, prefab, player build (~20 s)
python tools\ship\make_ship_plan.py corvette --check                                                                      # capture plans in the ship's own frame
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\capture.ps1 -Plan gauntlet\shots\corvette.json -Out gauntlet\captures\corvette\rN
python -I tools\sky\critic_view.py gauntlet\captures\corvette\rN                                                           # the critic's 1280 px JPEGs
```

## What lives where

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `tools\blender_lib\` | the kit, imported by every model script: `mntex` (numpy textures, trim atlas, PNG writer), `mnmesh` (meshes as arrays with explicit normals and two UV sets), `mnkit` (hull lofts, armour plating, trim strips, rings, fins, trusses, lights), `mnparts` and `mnsystems` (turrets, engines, sensors, comms, reactor, launchers, bridge: each with a wreck and debris), `mnship` (LODs, subsystems, hooks, damage sites, collision, FBX and manifest export), `mnpreview` (Workbench renders) |
| `art_src\<set>\build_*_textures.py` | a texture set (Earth's hull: plate, trim sheet, chrome, detail, lights, engine, plume, damage decals) and `trims.json`, the atlas rectangles the model scripts read |
| `art_src\<ship>\build_<ship>.py` | the model: composes kit calls, nothing hand-placed |
| `Game\Assets\Monolith\Art\<Set>\`, `...\<Ship>\` | output PNGs, `*.materials.json` (HDRP recipes), the FBX. Imported by `ShipArtImport` (suffix decides the settings) |
| `Game\Assets\Monolith\Resources\ShipArt\` | `<id>.ship.json` (manifest), `<id>.prefab`, `Materials\*.mat`: written by the model script and `ShipArtSetup`, loaded by `ShipArtCatalog` |
| `Game\Assets\Monolith\Scripts\ShipArt\` | runtime: `ShipSubsystem`, `ShipDamage` (`IShipDamage`), `ShipFxHook`, `HullDamageSite`, `ShipArtCatalog`, `ShipManifest` |

Never put `.blend` files under `Game\Assets`. Keep big sources out of git (over about 50 MB, ask the lead): the corvette set is 23 MB of PNG and a 5.5 MB FBX.

## How a ship is built (the hierarchy of scale)

Capital ships must read as enormous, so detail exists at every scale and is **designed, not noise**:
silhouette (10 to 100 m: lofted hull segments of chamfered rectangles joined by recessed necks and chrome collars, belts, decks, fins, trusses, radiator stacks, lances) ->
structure (2 to 10 m: armour plates in rows with gaps, each at its own height, some carrying smaller plates; ribs; turrets; nozzles) ->
detail (0.2 to 2 m: lit window rows, hatches, vents, conduits, fastener lines, light bars, stencils, running lights, all laid from the trim sheet) ->
surface (under 0.2 m: plate tile paint, scratches, chips, the tiling detail map on UV1 at 2 m).
The plates are real geometry with a chamfer, so every edge catches the Sun and panel lines are depth, not paint.

- **Axes while building:** x starboard, y bow, z up (Blender). `mnship.to_unity` is the one place that flips to Unity's (x right, y up, z forward). FBX settings are fixed by a probe (`mnship.py` top): Blender's axes untouched, no baking, Unity's *Bake Axis Conversion* on. `ShipArtSetup` fails loudly if a subsystem's position or facing in the prefab differs from the manifest.
- **UVs:** UV0 is metric tile coordinates for tiling materials (plate, chrome), an atlas rectangle for trim pieces. UV1 is always metric, for the detail map. Normals are explicit (flat faces own their vertices; lathes carry their own).
- **Materials (slots in the model script's list):** plate in five paint tones (deep black, graphite, mid, light and pale grey: five albedo tiles that share one normal and mask, so chips and scratches stay bare metal on every tone), trim (atlas: windows, hatches, vents, big panels, stencilled codes, chevrons, labels, edge paint, burnt metal), chrome (brushed tile, smoothness 0.95), lights (palette), engine (the dark funnel and the hot disc), plume (HDRP Unlit, additive), wear and paint (HDRP Lit, alpha blended). HDRP Lit mask map: R metallic, G occlusion, B detail mask, A smoothness. Albedo textures hold final values; the tone is in the texture, not in a tint.
- **Surface texture density (round 4):** the plate tile is 2,048 px per 12 m (5.9 mm a texel) and holds detail at every scale between a pixel and a plate (blotches, streaks, orange peel, round pits and dust, short scratches in all directions, irregular paint chips), with smoothness and occlusion varying with it; the detail map is 1,024 px per 2 m; the trim sheet is 4,096 px with every piece at 128 to 256 px a metre (`DK` in the texture script; the edge strips 384); the paint atlas 128 px a metre. Shapes in textures are never `Layers.rect` squares: use `soft_dot` (irregular, wraps for tiling) or the blob helpers. Smoothness differences are strong contrast at a grazing Sun: keep the plate's variation about +-0.06 or the lit faces look like camouflage. `ShowcaseOptions.hideMaterials` (e.g. `["MN_EarthWear", "MN_EarthPaint"]`) leaves layers out of a still so you can tell which one makes a mark (a hidden material shows magenta where it was).
- **Paint and wear at every scale (round 2):** `Kit.tones` gives each hull flat a dominant tone and its plates follow it with a spread plus a few accents (large scale: blocks of lighter and darker plating); every plate is its own height and takes its own part of the 12 m tile (mid scale); `Kit.scatter_details` puts a hatch, vent, stencilled code, chevron band or big access panel on most plates; `Kit.wear` lays alpha overlays one centimetre above a plate (grime or pale dust streaks along the ship, scorch blooms, edge scuffs, repainted patches, stains) chosen by the plate's tone (pale marks on dark paint, dark marks on pale paint); `trenches` leave channels in the plating with pipes in them; window rows are laid in broken runs with conduit between. No noise greebles.
- **Paint ageing by material and scale (round 3):** a second alpha-blended atlas, `earth_paint_albedo` (material `MN_EarthPaint`, trims.json key `paint`), holds paint that has aged *with* its coat: the stencilled codes, chevron band, hull number and emblem are solid pale paint that thins and chips (more at the strokes' edges), with primer ringing the holes and grime running below; none is an opaque rectangle with a border any more (`Kit.paint_quad`, laid over the plate). `Kit.edge_wear` lays a chip strip and a grime strip along each plate edge (chips are flakes of top paint gone to the primer with a scrape of bare metal inside, many at the edge and thinning inward, a worn metal line along it; grime gathers at the edge with short drips; dark plates show their chips, light plates collect grime). Plate chamfers and walls (the `edge_wall`, `edge_chamfer` trim strips) wear on their own: grime foot, primer then metal toward the lip. The wear atlas gained `bleach` (sun-bleached warm blotches on mid and dark paint); repaint patches are rare, soft-edged, roller-streaked and thin at one side (they used to be flat rectangles). Hardware pieces (hatches, vents) are aged too. `Kit.tones` keeps the tone spread narrow so the paint does not compete with the Sun's light and dark break.
- **Engine light on the hull (round 3):** the model script lists `ship.extra["engineLights"]` (per engine: distance behind the throat along the exhaust, candela, range); `ShipArtSetup` builds an HDRP point light `SPILL` (cold blue-white, no shadows) under each engine and `EngineGlow` drives it with the same flicker and `Throttle` as the flare (off when the engine is a wreck). The light falls on the engine block's flanks and sterns and the bell collars; it needs no probe or lightmap.
- **LODs:** `<id>_LOD0..2` built by the same function with `ctx.lod`: LOD0 everything; LOD1 carcass, strips and silhouette pieces without plate slabs (the subsystems keep their LOD0 meshes); LOD2 boxes. Screen heights in the manifest.
- **Collision:** `<id>_COL`, a few hundred triangles of coarse hull boxes (the prefab makes it a `MeshCollider`).
- **Check:** `Ship.export` refuses a mesh whose triangle winding disagrees with its normals.

## Subsystems, destruction and damage (the contract with combat)

Every part is its own named object, found by `ShipSubsystem` (kind, radius, hp, tags from the manifest):

```
SUB_<kind>_<name>                 pivot empty; +z is where it points. kinds: engine flak aaf launcher bridge sensors comms reactor
  __base, __head ...              the intact look (several meshes where parts move)
  __damaged                       optional half-wrecked look
  __destroyed                     the wreck: scorched stump, torn plates, embers
  __debris_NN                     detachable chunks, each with ejection velocity and spin in the manifest
FX_<kind>_<NN>                    where sparks, vents, fire and smoke may be spawned (ShipFxHook), +z out of the hull
DMG_site_<NN> (+ __breach)        a place on the hull that can be torn open (HullDamageSite); the breach mesh is hidden until hit
```

Engines are alive: `EngineGlow` (added to every `engine` subsystem by `ShipArtSetup`) flickers the hot core and the flare with smooth noise and scales the flare with `Throttle` (0 off, 1 full); the flight or effects system sets it. The flare is a part of its own (`__plume`) so a wreck loses it.

Combat calls `IShipDamage` (on the prefab root, `ShipDamage`), it needs no knowledge of the model:

```csharp
var dmg = shipObject.GetComponent<ShipDamage>();
dmg.SetSubsystemHealth("SUB_flak_S02", 0.3f);        // below 0.5: damaged look if the model has one; 0: wreck
dmg.DestroySubsystem("engine");                      // by name, or the first intact part of that kind
foreach (var (piece, v, spin) in dmg.Find("SUB_engine_C").DetachDebris()) { /* effects own the pieces now */ }
dmg.AddHullHit(worldPoint, worldNormal, radiusMetres, severity01);   // scorch decal (pooled HDRP decal projectors), opens the nearest site
dmg.SetHullDamage(0.6f);                             // progressive: a fixed plan of sampled hull points, more and bigger scorches, then breaches
foreach (var hook in dmg.Hooks("spark")) { /* spawn particles at hook.transform, along hook.Direction */ }
dmg.SubsystemChanged += (sub, from, to) => { /* explosion, radio line, objective */ };
```

Scorch decals come from one atlas (4 x 4: small scorches, blasts, craters, streaks). Damaged-state frame cost on the corvette: about 3% (see the piece history).

## Small ships: the fighter kit (`mnfighter`, `art_src\earth_fighters`)

Fighters (10 to 20 m) are built from the same kit at a different scale, with `toolslender_lib\mnfighter.py`: `solid()` (a bevelled, plated, flat-faced solid from a quad: wings, fins, pods; `lod2=False` leaves it out of LOD2), `hull()` (loft stations plus plating and rails), `taper`, `band`, `whip` (antennas), `collar`, `hoop`, and the subsystem builders `fighter_engine` (the capital bell, a fifth the size, with a short flare: `plume_len`), `needle_gun` (primary, `heavy=True` for the fat gun), `sensor_eye`, `missile_rack`, `comms_array`. `dark_paint()` swaps the paint batches for matte black tones; `PLATING` and `WEAR_STYLE` are the small-plate styles. `damage_points()` and `add_site()` place the scorch points and breach sites. One script per class (`build_earth_<class>.py`, `-- --preview` for Workbench renders under `_preview`, gitignored).

Conventions the fighters add to the contract:

- A gun mount is `SUB_mount_<name>` (kind `mount`), with the tag `gun:primary` or `gun:heavy` in the manifest; `ShipArtVisual` reads the tag to know which weapon's muzzle the part is, and `ShipCombatFactory` maps the kind to the combat flags (`PrimaryOffline` and `HeavyOffline`).
- A ship def (`Resources\Combat\ships\<id>.json`) says what a model part is worth with `partKinds` (hp as a percent of the hull per kind: engine, mount, sensors, launcher, comms). When it is present the data's stand-in `parts` are not used with a model; they stay for the headless lab, where there is no model.
- Manifest keys: `scorchScale` (hit radii are set for capital hulls; a fighter's scorch is a fraction of that), `damagePoints`, `lodScreenHeights`, `engineLights` (per engine: `z`, `candela`, `range`, `fade`, `radius`) and `engineFollowsShip` (the glow follows the world ship's throttle and afterburner; set by `ShipArtSetup` on `EngineGlow.FollowShip`).
- Wiring a model into a mission needs nothing: `GameMissionHost` uses `ShipArtCatalog.Exists(class)`. The hand-written scenes (`DogfightBattle.SpawnFighter`, `CombatRange.SpawnDrone` with the `model` and `def` fields in `range.json`, the juice sandbox slots) say it themselves. Hull break-up sections are baked by `JuiceSetup.BakeSections` for every ShipArt prefab (run it after `Setup.Project` whenever a model changes).

## The diaspora's ships (`mndiaspora`, `art_src\diaspora_hull`, `art_src\diaspora`)

Our side's look is the opposite of Earth's, from the same kit (design section 9): warm, patched and part living where Earth is black, chrome and perfect. Everything
comes through the same pipeline, so nothing in the game knows the difference except the material set the manifest names.

- **The texture set** (`art_src\diaspora_hull\build_diaspora_hull_textures.py`, about two minutes, `-- trim` and so on for one part, `-- none` only rewrites the HDRP
  recipes) is the Earth set re-graded and extended, with the **same atlas layout and trim piece names**, so every kit function runs on it unchanged. Five plate
  batches in the kit's tone order (`PLATE_B` rust oxide, `PLATE` bone-olive, `PLATE_C` olive drab, `PLATE_D` bare repair steel with its own normal and mask maps,
  `PLATE_E` faded teal), dull steel in the `CHROME` slot, the lamp palette's swatch 0 is warm white and swatch 1 the graft's teal, amber engines. Three more slots:
  `BIO` (12; the cabin's own dark ribbed texture), `GLYPH` (13; the cabin's glyph strip, emissive) and `GLASS` (14; a tinted opaque dome with a faint glow). New trim
  pieces: `glyph_a`, `glyph_b`, `hazard`, `weld`, `cable`, `container_a/b/c`, `deck`, `name_mercy`; the atlas dropped four Earth pieces the diaspora does not use (radiator,
  heat tiles, sensor grid, chrome panel) to make room. The damage decals, the detail map, the bio and the glyph textures are copies.
- **The kit** (`tools\blender_lib\mndiaspora.py`, capital parts in `mndiaspora_cap.py`): `set_paint()` (the per-region mix of batches; call before building),
  `sweep` (a smooth tube along a path: veins, ribs, cables), `ribbed_shell` and `bio_hoop` (the dark grown shells; `lod=2` is a plain cone), `repair_patch` (plates welded over
  damage with a bead and bolts), `pipe_run`, `container`, `hazard_band`, `glyph_band`, `weld`; small-ship parts (`sensor_eye`, `needle_gun`, `fighter_engine`) and
  capital parts (`gun_turret`, `beam_lance`, `hangar_bay`, `reactor_heart`, `bridge_pod`, `sensor_crest`, `bio_fin`). `mnship.Ship` is given `mndiaspora.MATERIALS`
  (15 names, the first twelve in the capital kit's order).
- **The models**: `art_src\diaspora\build_vanguard_fighter.py` (the player's fighter and the Sable wingmen; its nose pod is the cabin's arch and canopy, taken
  from `art_src\cabin\build_cabin.py`) and `build_diaspora_cruiser.py` (the Small Mercy). Run each with `tools\blender.ps1 <script> [-- --preview]`; Workbench renders
  go to `art_src\diaspora\_preview` (the flat preview colours are in `mnpreview.DEFAULT_COLOURS`; the previews understate the game's look, the paint overlay shows
  as ochre blocks). The ship id is the **class id** (`vanguard_fighter`, `diaspora_cruiser`), so `GameMissionHost` picks the model by itself.
- **Conventions added to the contract**: a combat ship def with a model lists `partKinds` (hp per kind) and keeps its old `parts` as stand-ins for the headless lab
  (`ShipCombatFactory.AddModelParts` swaps them when a model is there); kinds the def does not list (the Mercy's `hangar` bays) are decorative but destructible through `ShipDamage`,
  and the juice's staged capital death takes them one after another like any part. The player's own ship is never drawn, so it keeps the stand-in parts.
- **After any model change**: `Setup.Project` (prefabs), `JuiceSetup.BakeSections` (the hull break-up cuts for every ShipArt prefab), then `Build.Player`.
- **Captures**: `tools\ship\make_diaspora_plan.py` (every ship alone in three lighting sets, damage, the finger-four, the Mercy next to Earth's corvette) and
  `make_diaspora_action.py` (spectator cameras on a Sable wingman and on the Mercy in the dogfight); `gauntlet\missions\mercy_death_test.mission.json` starts the Mercy at 2 percent
  hull for the staged-death frames (a fixed camera: the spectator camera hands back to the cockpit when its ship dies).

## The Oort models: a comet outpost and a destroyer (`mnrock`, `mnearth`, `mnheavy`, `art_src\oort_art`)

Earth's two big pieces for the Oort mission, built on the capital kit and three more libraries; the ship id is the class id, so the mission picks the model up by class with no change.

- **`mnrock`** (numpy only): a comet nucleus is a smooth union of superellipsoid lobes found by marching rays from the centre (the outermost root), then displaced along the ray by domain-warped
  noise (lumps, ridges, strata only in "cliff" zones, craters) and carved by edit functions the model script gives it (a flat shelf for the station, a cliff and a pocket for the hangar, pits for
  the guns, vents for the jets). `RockMesh` answers `drop(x, y)` and `ray(o, d)` by casting against the mesh itself, so the station stands on the rock that is really there. UVs are box-projected
  per face from a normal smoothed over about 60 m (few, long seams), UV1 metric for the tiling detail map. `boulder()` adds loose rocks (a displaced icosphere sunk into the surface).
  Levels: 6 is 82k triangles with 9 m edges (LOD0), 5 the distance mesh, 3 the collision proxy; the lobes and craters agree between them.
- **`mnearth`**: what Earth's big builds share: `loft_placed` (a hull segment lofted in a frame of its own and put in ship space with its plating patches, so a spine can run along x), plating
  bands whose window rows work on either winding of a flank and scale with the ship (`win_scale`), chrome rails, a lattice tower, a clamp claw. **`mnheavy`**: the beam cannon (a barbette, a
  turret body with radiators, a caged barrel of chrome coil rings with red bands, a red lens; the subsystem sits at the MUZZLE because the juice starts the charge glow and the beam at the part's
  position) and the chin hangar (a plated pod with its far face open, lit ceiling strips, doors hung open).
- **Textures** (`build_oort_textures.py`, into `Art\EarthHull` so one `earth_oort.materials.json` can name both sets): the crust ("popcorn" lumps at three scales, sparse long fractures, dust,
  pits with rims, ice specks), the frost, a jet atlas (four nested shells, edges inset against bilinear wrap), a detail map. They must be statistically uniform: the mesh is box-projected and the
  tile repeats every 160 m, so anything bigger than a few metres in the albedo shows as a grid (the first version had Voronoi cells and a quilted look).
- **Materials**: `MN_OortRock` (dark crust), `MN_OortIce` (frost: pits, vents, big crater floors), `MN_OortJet` (HDRP Unlit additive, like the plumes; `ShipArtSetup` turns shadows off for it).
  Jets and vents are decorative subsystems (kind `vent`, not in `partKinds`): they break for the show and stay out of the juice's hull sections.
- **Combat wiring** (`wire_combat_defs.py`, plain Python): adds `partKinds` to the two defs and replaces their `geometry` boxes with ones that follow the model (the capital AI's turret arcs are
  measured off the nearest box face, so a gun must sit near a box whose face points the way it should fire; the script prints each part's normal). Cannon boxes cover their whole barrel.
- **Beam sweeps and hit boxes**: a capital's beam sweeps a line along the face of the def's boxes' bounding box at 0.9 of its half-extent and only hurts what the line meets, so a big irregular body's
  boxes must fill that face (the outpost's nucleus is one broad box at 0.95 of its bounds; inscribed lobe boxes made the Patience's beams miss and the outpost fall 3 minutes late).
- **Death**: the juice's capital death works on both as it is (parts go one after another, the baked hull sections fly apart, a final blast sized to the hull, the shockwave); for the nucleus the
  sections are slabs of rock with the station's parts riding the nearest slab. `gauntlet\missions\{outpost,archon}_death_test.mission.json` (target at 2 percent hull, the Mercy's beams free) and
  `tools\ship\make_oortart_plan.py outpost_death|archon_death` make the death captures; `archon_test.mission.json` is the lab for the scale and beam shots.

## The Oort ice field's comets (`mncomet`, `art_src\comets`, `art_src\oort_art\build_comet_textures.py`)

Five free-floating nuclei the Oort mission's scenery scatters by the hundred (`Game/Mission/CometField`, which replaced the generated icospheres of `IceRocks`; piece notes in `gauntlet/pieces/comets/history.md`).
Not ships: no subsystems, no collision, no damage; the scenery is drawn only.

```
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\blender.ps1 art_src\oort_art\build_comet_textures.py   # ~45 s: crust, dust, detail (2,048 / 1,024 px) and comets.materials.json
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\blender.ps1 art_src\comets\build_comets.py             # ~1 min: five FBXs and Resources\CometArt\comets.json  (-- --only a,b --preview a --level 6 --no-export)
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\unity.ps1 Monolith.EditorTools.CometArtSetup.Run        # the layered material and a prefab with a LOD group per model (Setup.Project calls it too)
```

- **`mncomet`** (numpy only, deterministic): a `Shape` (a smooth union of superellipsoid lobes, optionally cut by planes; the radius along each icosphere direction is found by marching, as in `mnrock`) and a
  `Terrain` laid on it along the shape's own normal: lumps, rough and smooth provinces, terraces and scarps that rise over a set number of metres (cliffs of a chosen steepness, wherever the noise's
  gradient lies), craters with flat dusty floors and sharp rims, steep terraced pits, fractures, `rubble` (sharp-crested noise on a ladder of wavelengths), dust that fills every hollow, then boulders
  (thick at the foot of cliffs and in hollows) and ice sites. Every level of detail (icosphere 7, 6, 5, 4, 3: 330k to 1.3k triangles) is built from the same fields, so the silhouettes agree.
- **One layered surface material** (HDRP LayeredLit, `MN_CometSurface`): layer 0 the crust, 1 the dust, 2 the frost (the outpost's ice maps); the mesh's vertex colour is the blend (R dust, G ice), so a patch has no
  edge a face can show. The UVs are box-projected (UV0 metres / 150, UV1 metres / 4, vertices shared between faces of one plane), the textures statistically uniform so a tile never shows.
  `CometArtSetup` makes the material from `comets.materials.json` (the `layers` recipe) and the prefabs from the manifest: LOD heights per model, no collider, shadows on.
- **Sizes**: a model is built at its own size (the two large ones ~1.2 km, the small ones ~250 m) and centred on its volume; the manifest's `bound` (the farthest vertex) and `fit` (how far a rock of
  the mission's radius should reach, in radii) let the game scale it so a rock of radius R fits a sphere of about `fit` x R.
- **Looks**: `-- --preview a,b` renders Cycles stills (one hard Sun, a black sky, vertex colours as the game blends them) into `art_src\comets\_preview` (gitignored): the geometry in seconds, the real look is the game's.

## Adding a ship

1. Copy `art_src\earth_corvette`, rename. Reuse `earth_hull` textures for Earth ships; a new faction gets its own `art_src\<set>` script (copy it, change the look: albedo, trim pieces, markings, emissive colours).
2. Describe the hull as stations (`station(y, hw, hh, chamfer)`), plate each band (`plate_bands`), add rows of windows, collars, fins, then subsystems with `ctx.subsystem(name, kind, frame, radius)` and the part builders. Add a new part builder to `mnparts`/`mnsystems` with an intact look, a wreck and debris: it will be there for the next ship.
3. Run the model script, `Setup.All`, a capture. Look at `--preview` Workbench renders first: they take 10 s and show silhouette and layering.
4. Write the ship's `monolith-ship-class/1` flight data separately (`Resources\Ships\<id>.json`): the model gives only size, subsystems and lights.

## The cabin (`art_src\cabin`)

The pilot's cabin (there are no hands: the user dropped them), built by the same kit (`tools\blender_lib\mncabin.py` for the cabin's pieces: chamfered boxes, tubes, lofts, trim plates and screens; `mnpaint.py` for the
textures; `mnpreview` renders Workbench views from inside). Run in this order:

```
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\blender.ps1 art_src\cabin\build_cabin_textures.py     # the texture set: plate, metal, floor, fabric, bio, glyph, tissue, rubber, trim sheet, screen
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\blender.ps1 art_src\cabin\build_cabin.py             # ~3 s: cabin.fbx, cabin.layout.json (`-- --preview` renders views into $MN_PREVIEW_DIR, art_src\cabin\_preview by default)
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\unity.ps1 Monolith.EditorTools.Setup.All               # CabinArtSetup: materials, prefabs, player build
```

- **Axes:** x starboard, y forward (the canopy), z up, in metres, the seated eye at y = 0; the layout JSON is in Unity's axes. The layout is data, never code: the floor levels, obstacles,
  stand points, interactables, lights and displays are in `Resources\Cabin\cabin.layout.json` (written by the model script) and `CabinLayout` reads them. Stand points are where the pilot stands to work each one (the work shows on the cabin itself;
  the pilot never ducks and has no hands: the `crouch` and `grip` data the generator still writes are not read).
- **Study, not copy:** Soyuz, Apollo and the ISS for the cramped, rough, utilitarian worn human hull (structural ribs every half metre with panels between, handrails, tied cable bundles,
  labelled switch banks, placards, worn paint at every touch point, hatches with wheels, caged lamps); the dark, ribbed, wet-looking, glyph-lit biomechanical grafts of the alien-ally
  technology in *Independence Day* kept rough on top. Never bizarre, never clean.
- **Contract with the code (`Monolith.Cabin`):** node names are looked up by `CabinRig`, and the self-test refuses a prefab that lacks one:
  `DISPLAY_<name>` (a quad facing the viewer: the table, the two dash screens, the breaker, the rack labels), `ANCHOR_*` (grips and view points), `BREAKER_LEVER`, `PANEL_<engine|arms>`
  with `_DOOR`, `_MODULE`, `_KNIT`, `RACK_MODULE_<gun0|gun1|msl0|msl1>`, `CABIN_GLASS` and the canopy frame.
- **Materials** are recipes in `cabin.materials.json` that `CabinArtSetup` turns into editor-made HDRP Lit assets under `Resources\Cabin\Materials` (runtime materials are never made with
  `Shader.Find`); the displays' `MN_CabScreen` is one emissive asset that `CabinRig` instances and gives a render texture each. Every renderer is on HDRP light layer 1.
- **Size:** the texture set is about 42 MB of PNG (2,048 px tiles, the plate a 2 m tile at about a millimetre a pixel), the FBX 1.6 MB: under the 50 MB line, but the biggest art set in the repo.

## Gotchas

- Blind look-test stills must carry no HUD: the `corvette` scene builds none (no `FlightHud`, no `CockpitView`); keep it that way for any new showcase scene.
- A heredoc with big Python in the bash tool can fail to parse: write patch scripts to a file.
- The capture scene `"scene": "corvette"` (`ShipShowcase`) takes `sceneOptions` for any ship id: orientation (the Sun is fixed in the sky, so yaw/pitch/roll decide the light; `make_ship_plan.py` solves them and the player logs `SHOWCASE sun in the ship's frame` so a wrong matrix shows at once), a placeholder fighter, damage, and `sky` (any `SkyOptions` field by name, for look development without a rebuild). It uses `SkyRig` as the game does and builds no HUD.
- Shadows and occlusion are the shared profile (`Monolith.RenderQuality`, built by `SkyRig`): 4 cascades of 4,096 ending 60 m, 240 m, 960 m and 6 km out, contact shadows, SSAO, micro shadows. Micro shadows use the plate's mask-map green channel (AO), so a plate texture's AO is not decoration: it shadows the Sun's light across fine relief. Do not give a scene its own shadow volume; override `Quality` in the plan's `sky` options or with `-mnQuality`. Round 3's captures had 512-pixel cascades and no AO, which is why the hull read "flat".
- Lens ghosts (`LookOptions.Ghosts`) are the Sun reflected between lens surfaces, so `CameraLook` fades them with the Sun's distance outside the frame (`GhostReach`, 0.45: gone by half a frame beyond the edge). In ship shots with the Sun out of the picture they were sprites that matched nothing lighting the hull; with the Sun in or near the picture they are unchanged.
- Unity applies the textbook rotation matrices to its axes (`Quaternion.Euler` = Ry * Rx * Rz, +pitch points the nose down): `make_ship_plan.rot` follows that. Round 1's plans had Rx backwards, so its Sun was not where its notes said.
- `MaterialPropertyBlock.SetColor` gamma-converts a value, which turns 150,000 nits into white: set emissive nits with `SetVector` (EngineGlow does).
- Screen-space reflections (`SkyOptions.ScreenSpaceReflections`; needs `supportSSR` in the HDRP asset, which `Hdrp.EnsureAsset` sets) are what make polished metal reflect the lit plates beside it. Changing a model changes the juice's break-up sections: run `tools/unity.ps1 Monolith.EditorTools.JuiceSetup.BakeSections` after `Setup.All`.
- Emissive: nits at fixed EV 14 (`earth_hull.materials.json`): windows 42,000, lights 150,000, engine disc 150,000, plume 48,000 (the core column at full strength, the shells outside it a fifth to a twentieth), decal embers 14,000. Bloom starts around 17,700 nits.
- HDRP decal emissive and DBuffer need nothing in the player settings, but the decal material must be an asset (it is: `MN_EarthDamageDecal`).
- `_UVDetailsMappingMask` is set by the material inspector in the editor, not by `ResetMaterialKeywords`: `ShipArtSetup` sets it itself.
